Newly released Epstein documents reveal that draft wills from 2012-2014 named former Treasury Secretary Larry Summers and former Barclays CEO Jes Staley as "successor executors" of Jeffrey Epstein's estate, with each entitled to $250,000 upon completion of probate.

Summers served as Treasury Secretary under Clinton (1999-2001), World Bank Chief Economist (1991-1993), Director of the National Economic Council under Obama, and Harvard University president. He subsequently stepped down from his Harvard teaching post and his position on the OpenAI board following the revelations. Both Summers and Staley claimed no knowledge of being named in the will.

Staley served as Epstein's private banker at JP Morgan for over 30 years. He was banned from the UK financial industry in 2021 for lying to regulators about his relationship with Epstein, which he had characterized as "purely professional." The documents reveal the relationship was close enough for Epstein to entrust his estate to Staley's management.

Neither Summers nor Staley appeared in Epstein's final 2019 will executed shortly before his death. The documents briefly appeared on the DOJ website before being removed without explanation, then restored after public backlash.

The revelations demonstrate the depth of Epstein's connections to the highest levels of American financial and political elite—individuals who held the most powerful economic policy positions in the U.S. government while maintaining relationships with a convicted sex offender close enough to be named estate executors.
