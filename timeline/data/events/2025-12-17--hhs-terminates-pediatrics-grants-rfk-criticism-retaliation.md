The Department of Health and Human Services abruptly terminated seven federal grants totaling nearly $12 million to the American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP). The canceled grants supported critical programs including sudden infant death reduction, teen health improvement, birth defect prevention including fetal alcohol syndrome, and early autism identification.

HHS justified the terminations by claiming grants "no longer align with the Department's mission or priorities" and cited concerns about "identity-based language" in grant materials. The AAP, representing 67,000 pediatricians nationwide, had publicly criticized HHS Secretary RFK Kennedy Jr.'s vaccine policies, including his dismissal of the CDC's vaccine advisory panel.

The AAP filed a federal lawsuit on December 24, 2025, alleging the grant terminations violated First Amendment rights and constituted retaliation for opposing administration public health policies. Prior to the cancellations, AAP had received $18.4 million in federal funding from HHS in 2025.

The termination represents weaponization of federal funding to punish scientific organizations that criticize administration health policies. Programs serving vulnerable children—autism screening, SIDS prevention, birth defect reduction—were eliminated as apparent punishment for the AAP's defense of evidence-based medicine.
