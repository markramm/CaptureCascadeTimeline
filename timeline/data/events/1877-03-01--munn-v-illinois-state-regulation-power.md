The U.S. Supreme Court ruled 7-2 in Munn v. Illinois that states have constitutional authority to regulate private businesses "affected with a public interest" under their police powers. Chief Justice Morrison Waite's majority opinion upheld Illinois's 1871 law setting maximum rates for grain storage and transport, rejecting the warehouse firm's argument that rate regulation violated the Fourteenth Amendment's Due Process Clause. This decision established a critical precedent allowing states to regulate corporations serving the public, directly responding to pressure from the National Grange farmers' association. The ruling temporarily affirmed state regulatory power over corporate interests during the Gilded Age, though subsequent corporate personhood cases would erode this authority. Munn represents a high-water mark for state regulation before the court's later expansion of corporate constitutional protections.
