EPA Administrator Lee Zeldin announced proposed rules to repeal both mercury emission standards for power plants and carbon pollution limits, representing the most significant environmental deregulation in decades. The proposal would eliminate protections that have prevented an estimated 11,000 premature deaths annually from mercury and other toxic pollutants.

The Mercury and Air Toxics Standards (MATS), established in 2012, represented the first federal limits on mercury, arsenic, and acid gases from power plants. The carbon rules, finalized in 2024, required coal plants to capture 90% of emissions or retire. Both rules faced industry opposition despite broad public health benefits.

Zeldin described the effort as driving "a dagger through the heart of the climate-change religion," revealing the ideological rather than scientific basis for the rollback. Coal industry lobbyists celebrated the announcement, having advocated for precisely these changes through industry groups that donated heavily to Trump's campaign and inauguration.

The proposal exemplifies regulatory capture: industries being regulated effectively write the rules. Coal executives and lobbyists staffed key EPA positions, then immediately moved to eliminate regulations affecting their former employers. Public health impacts will fall disproportionately on communities near coal plants, often lower-income and minority populations.
