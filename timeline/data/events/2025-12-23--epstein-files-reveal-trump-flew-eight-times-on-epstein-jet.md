A 2020 federal prosecutor email released as part of the Epstein document disclosure reveals that Donald Trump flew on Jeffrey Epstein's private jet "many more times than previously has been reported." Flight records show Trump as a passenger on at least 8 flights between 1993 and 1996—far more than the 1-2 flights previously publicly known.

Four of these flights included Ghislaine Maxwell as a fellow passenger. One flight included only Trump, Epstein, and a 20-year-old woman whose name remains redacted in the released documents. Other flights included Trump with his then-wife Marla Maples and children Tiffany and Eric.

The documents reveal that federal prosecutors possessed this information in 2020 but it was not previously disclosed to the public—meaning this evidence existed through Trump's first presidency, the 2020 election, and into his second term. The information only emerged due to the congressional mandate in the bipartisan Epstein Files Transparency Act.

The pattern demonstrates institutional protection of a powerful political figure: prosecutors held evidence of a more extensive Trump-Epstein relationship than publicly acknowledged, but this information was suppressed until Congress forced its release. Trump is now president while his DOJ controls what Epstein documents are released and how heavily they are redacted.
