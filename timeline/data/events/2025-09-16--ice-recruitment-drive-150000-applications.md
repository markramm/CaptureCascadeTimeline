DHS announced that ICE received over 150,000 applications from Americans seeking to join immigration enforcement, with 18,000 tentative job offers extended. The Trump administration used congressional funding to recruit 10,000 new ICE agents, offering bonuses up to $50,000, $60,000 in student loan repayment, and relaxed age restrictions.

The recruitment drive represents the largest expansion of immigration enforcement capacity in American history. DHS characterized applicants as patriotic Americans eager to help "remove the worst criminal aliens," framing immigration enforcement as a popular cause.

Critics noted the rapid expansion prioritizes quantity over quality, potentially leading to more aggressive enforcement with less oversight. The relaxed hiring standards and financial incentives could attract applicants more interested in the benefits than in following legal constraints on enforcement.

The 150,000 applications—far exceeding the 10,000 positions available—demonstrated significant public appetite for immigration enforcement jobs. Civil liberties advocates warned that the dramatic capacity expansion, combined with weakened due process protections, would enable enforcement far beyond what existing legal frameworks were designed to support.
