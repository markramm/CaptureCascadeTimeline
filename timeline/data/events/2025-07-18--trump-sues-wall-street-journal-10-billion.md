President Trump filed a $10 billion defamation lawsuit against the Wall Street Journal, its parent company Dow Jones, News Corp, Rupert Murdoch, and two individual journalists. The suit represented the largest media lawsuit ever filed by a sitting president.

The lawsuit targeted critical reporting on Trump's business dealings and conflicts of interest, claiming the coverage was defamatory and intended to harm his reputation. Legal experts noted the claims faced significant First Amendment hurdles but that the lawsuit's purpose was intimidation rather than winning.

The suit against Murdoch-owned properties was particularly notable given Murdoch's role as a longtime Trump ally. The filing suggested even previously friendly media outlets faced legal retaliation for critical coverage, signaling that no media organization could safely report unfavorable stories.

Press freedom advocates condemned the lawsuit as a Strategic Lawsuit Against Public Participation (SLAPP) designed to chill journalism through the cost and burden of litigation. Even if Trump eventually lost, the lawsuit would impose millions in legal costs and create uncertainty that could affect editorial decisions across the media industry.
