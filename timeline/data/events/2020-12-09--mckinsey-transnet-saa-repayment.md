McKinsey & Company agrees to repay the entirety of fees earned on projects for Transnet and South African Airways (SAA) brought into disrepute by the involvement of Regiments Capital, totaling approximately R650 million ($40 million). This repayment comes in addition to the R1 billion ($74 million) McKinsey already returned to Eskom in 2018, bringing total South African repayments to approximately R1.65 billion. Transnet later argues the amount should be higher, resulting in a final settlement of R870 million ($63 million) to the state logistics company. The State Capture Commission heard extensive evidence about McKinsey's partnership with Regiments and Trillian, two local consulting firms closely linked to the Gupta-Zuma corruption network. These firms were hired to do financial advisory work for major state-owned enterprises, with senior officials abusing their positions to award contracts from which the Guptas earned billions in kickbacks. McKinsey partner Vikas Sagar, who ran the South African office, was allegedly colluding with Gupta henchman Salim Essa. McKinsey joins the ranks of KPMG, SAP and Bain & Co in being caught in the state capture scandal. However, unlike Bain which ultimately exits South Africa, McKinsey continues operating in the country. McKinsey characterizes the cloud hanging over it as an oversight in due diligence rather than admitting involvement in state capture or corruption. After being caught in the Gupta State Capture web, McKinsey implements stricter controls across its global operations including requiring company permission to give clients gifts, capping fees, and establishing a global hotline for reporting corrupt activity.
