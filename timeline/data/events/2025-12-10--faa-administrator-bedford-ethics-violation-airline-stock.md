The Office of Government Ethics confirmed that FAA Administrator Bryan Bedford violated his ethics agreement by failing to divest between $6 million and $30 million in Republic Airways stock within 90 days of his July 9, 2025 confirmation. Over 150 days after confirmation, he still held the stock.

Senator Maria Cantwell, ranking member of the Commerce Committee, revealed that Bedford attempted to amend his ethics agreement in October but was denied by OGE for not meeting standards. Bedford only requested stock certificates to facilitate divestment on December 1, 2025—months after the deadline.

The timing created significant conflicts: Republic Airways merged with Mesa Air Group on November 25, 2025, making Bedford's shares worth approximately 88% of the combined publicly-traded company. As FAA Administrator, Bedford oversees regulations affecting the airline industry including his former employer.

The violation exemplifies how Trump administration officials maintain financial interests in industries they regulate, creating inherent conflicts between their regulatory duties and personal wealth. Despite the confirmed ethics violation, Bedford remains in his position overseeing aviation safety.
