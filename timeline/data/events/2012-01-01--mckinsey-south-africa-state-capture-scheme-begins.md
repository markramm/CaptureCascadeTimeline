McKinsey Africa enters into corrupt partnerships with South African consulting firms that will pay bribes to officials at Transnet (South Africa's state-owned ports, rails, and pipelines company) and Eskom (the state-owned energy company) to secure lucrative consulting contracts. Senior McKinsey partner Vikas Sagar colludes with Gupta family lieutenant Salim Essa to sign supplier development contracts between McKinsey and local firms Regiments and later Trillian management consultants.

These contracts become the conduit for Essa and the Gupta family to extract and launder multimillions of rand in kickbacks from Transnet, Eskom, and South African Airways as part of the broader 'state capture' scheme that hollows out South African government institutions. McKinsey obtains sensitive confidential and non-public information from these state-owned enterprises regarding the award of consulting contracts and submits proposals for multimillion-dollar consulting engagements while knowing that their local partners will pay a portion of fees as bribes to government officials.

McKinsey's global reputation is leveraged to shield local partners Regiments and Trillian from scrutiny, allowing them to embed themselves inside state-owned companies and channel billions into Gupta-linked laundering networks. The consulting contracts are designed to appear legitimate while functioning as vehicles for systematic corruption and looting of public resources.

This marks the beginning of a five-year bribery scheme through which McKinsey and McKinsey Africa will earn approximately $85 million in profits. The scheme represents how elite Western consulting firms enable and profit from corruption in developing countries, using their prestigious brands to legitimize relationships with corrupt intermediaries who pay bribes on their behalf.
