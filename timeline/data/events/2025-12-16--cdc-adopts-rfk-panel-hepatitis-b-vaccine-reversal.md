The CDC officially adopts a recommendation from RFK Jr.'s handpicked Advisory Committee on Immunization Practices (ACIP) to drop the 30-year policy that all newborns be vaccinated against hepatitis B. The panel voted 8-3 on December 5 to weaken the recommendation, with the CDC finalizing adoption on December 16.

This represents the first major undermining of childhood vaccination protections based on ideology rather than science. Kennedy fired all 17 original ACIP members in June 2025 and replaced them with allies, many with ties to vaccine-skeptical groups. The new recommendation calls for "individual decision-making" for babies whose mothers test negative, with first doses delayed to at least 2 months if not given at birth.

The American Medical Association called the decision "reckless," stating it "undermines decades of public confidence in a proven, lifesaving vaccine" and "creates confusion for parents." Legal experts note the process may violate the Administrative Procedure Act, which requires open, deliberative processes based on scientific evidence.

Hours after the vote, Trump issued an executive order directing federal health officials to review the entire childhood immunization schedule and consider recommending fewer shots. An ACIP working group is currently reviewing all childhood vaccines for potential additional changes, signaling this is just the beginning of a broader rollback of vaccination recommendations.
