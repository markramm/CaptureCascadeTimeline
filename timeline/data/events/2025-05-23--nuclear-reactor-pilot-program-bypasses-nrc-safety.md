President Trump signed Executive Order 14301 establishing a DOE Reactor Pilot Program that bypasses Nuclear Regulatory Commission oversight for advanced nuclear reactors. The program directs DOE to approve at least three reactors with a goal of achieving criticality by July 4, 2026—America's 250th birthday.

The order leverages DOE authority under the Atomic Energy Act which exempts reactors built "under contract with and for the account of" the department from NRC licensing requirements. DOE selected 11 advanced reactor projects in August 2025, including Oklo Inc.'s Aurora reactor—previously denied NRC approval in 2022 due to safety documentation gaps.

Under the program, the NRC serves only in a consulting role while DOE's Office of Nuclear Energy runs approvals. Critics warn the July 2026 deadline creates dangerous pressure, with AI safety expert Heidy Khlaaf stating "these manufactured timelines are actually incredibly concerning. There's no timeline for assessing a new design and making sure it's safe."

The program represents a fundamental shift from 50 years of independent NRC safety oversight of nuclear facilities. Reactors that could not meet NRC standards can now be approved through DOE's expedited process, prioritizing political symbolism over safety analysis.
