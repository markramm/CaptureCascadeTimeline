Attorney General nominee Pam Bondi faced questioning at her Senate Judiciary Committee confirmation hearing about her extensive corporate lobbying work since leaving as Florida Attorney General, including representing Amazon, Uber, Qatar, and private prison company GEO Group.

Bondi registered as a foreign agent for Qatar in 2020, receiving over $1 million for lobbying work. She also lobbied for GEO Group, one of the nation's largest private prison operators that stands to profit enormously from the Trump administration's mass deportation policies. Amazon and Uber retained her while facing various regulatory and antitrust scrutiny.

When pressed on how she would handle conflicts between her former clients and DOJ enforcement priorities, Bondi provided evasive answers and failed to commit to specific recusals. She deflected questions about whether she would pursue political prosecutions by repeatedly referencing her decades of prosecutorial experience without addressing the substance.

The hearing exemplified the revolving door between corporate lobbying and government service that enables regulatory capture. Critics noted the fundamental conflict of an Attorney General who recently lobbied for major corporations now overseeing federal enforcement against those same companies and industries.
