The Trump administration fired the entire staff of the Low Income Home Energy Assistance Program (LIHEAP) at the Department of Health and Human Services on April 1, 2025. The move came as LIHEAP funding was reduced from $6.1 billion in FY2023 to $4.1 billion in FY2025—a cut of approximately one-third.

Households simultaneously face a 9.2% increase in winter heating costs, with the average household spending $995 this winter. The National Energy Assistance Directors Association warned that reduced funding combined with rising costs will leave millions of low-income families unable to afford heat.

The administration proposed eliminating LIHEAP funding entirely in FY2026. LIHEAP helps approximately 6 million low-income households annually pay heating and cooling bills, preventing utility shutoffs and helping families avoid choosing between heat and food or medicine.

The firing of program staff signals intent to dismantle the program rather than simply reduce funding. Without staff to administer grants to states, the program cannot function even if funding is restored. The action represents deliberate destruction of infrastructure serving vulnerable populations.
