Catherine Engelbrecht co-founded True the Vote as an offshoot of the King Street Patriots, a Tea Party organization, after becoming 'sickened' by the election of President Barack Obama. The organization began training poll watchers to look for alleged voting irregularities, drawing immediate accusations of voter intimidation. In the 2010 election, True the Vote trained some 700 poll watchers who would 'hover behind people as they cast their ballots,' creating an intimidating presence at polling places. The organization rapidly expanded, claiming to organize poll-watching volunteers in 35 states while pushing for voter roll purges. In 2012, Democratic Congressman Elijah Cummings opened an investigation, stating: 'At some point, an effort to challenge voter registrations by the thousands without any legitimate basis may be evidence of illegal voter suppression. If these efforts are intentional, politically motivated and widespread across multiple states, they could amount to a criminal conspiracy to deny legitimate voters their constitutional rights.' After the 2020 election, True the Vote launched the largest mass voter challenge effort in Georgia history, targeting over 364,000 Georgians—many of whom were Black, brown, or first-time voters—and offered a $1 million 'bounty' for voter fraud reports while recruiting Navy SEALs to confront voters and poll workers. In 2024, True the Vote acknowledged to a Georgia judge that it actually had no evidence of voter fraud in the 2020 election, despite raising millions in donations based on such claims.
