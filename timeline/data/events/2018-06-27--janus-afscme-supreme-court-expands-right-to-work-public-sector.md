The Supreme Court rules 5-4 in Janus v. American Federation of State, County, and Municipal Employees that public sector unions cannot collect fees from non-union members, overturning the 1977 Abood v. Detroit Board of Education precedent and effectively imposing "right-to-work" on all public sector workers nationwide. Justice Samuel Alito, joined by Chief Justice Roberts and Justices Kennedy, Thomas, and Gorsuch—all with Federalist Society connections—holds that mandatory agency fees violate the First Amendment by compelling non-members to subsidize union speech on matters of public concern.

The decision affects approximately 5 million public sector workers across the country who were previously required to pay "fair share" fees covering the costs of collective bargaining representation, even if they chose not to join the union. By prohibiting unions from collecting any fees without "affirmative consent," the ruling significantly undermines public sector union finances and bargaining power, completing the judicial protection of the labor destruction campaign coordinated through ALEC right-to-work legislation at the state level.

The case was brought by the Liberty Justice Center, a conservative litigation organization, with Mark Janus—an Illinois child support specialist—as plaintiff. The ruling demonstrates how judicial capture through Federalist Society-vetted appointments enables and protects corporate labor suppression strategies, using First Amendment doctrine to dismantle worker collective power while corporate political spending receives unlimited First Amendment protection under Citizens United.
