Secretary of State Marco Rubio announced the elimination of 5,200 of USAID's 6,200 programs—an 83% reduction—effectively dismantling American humanitarian and development infrastructure built over decades. On the same day, a federal judge ruled that Trump had overstepped his constitutional authority in implementing the cuts.

The eliminated programs include disaster relief, food security initiatives, democracy promotion, global health programs including HIV/AIDS treatment, and maternal and child health services. Rubio framed the cuts as eliminating "waste" and refocusing on American interests, though critics noted the programs served strategic diplomatic purposes.

The judge's ruling found that the administration had violated the Impoundment Control Act by withholding congressionally appropriated funds. However, the administration continued implementing cuts while appealing, leaving programs and their beneficiaries in limbo.

The dismantling of USAID represents the most significant reduction in American soft power capacity in decades. Programs that took years to build and train local partners cannot be quickly reconstituted, meaning the cuts will have lasting effects on American influence and humanitarian capacity even if later administrations attempt to reverse them.
