Edwin Feulner becomes president of the Heritage Foundation, transforming it from a struggling startup with nine employees and four presidents in four years into the most influential conservative think tank in America. Feulner, leaving Representative Crane's office to take the position, immediately implements an aggressive, market-driven approach that abandons traditional think tank insularity in favor of accessible, concise policy studies designed for immediate political impact. Within eighteen months of assuming leadership, Feulner increases Heritage's budget from approximately $1 million to $2.5 million and expands the donor pool from a few thousand to about 120,000, demonstrating the effectiveness of direct-mail fundraising and targeted outreach to conservative activists. His leadership model integrates think tank research with real-time political strategy, creating the template for modern conservative advocacy organizations. Feulner's approach perfectly implements Powell's vision of aggressive political engagement, positioning Heritage to produce the "Mandate for Leadership" that will shape the Reagan administration and establishing the organizational infrastructure that will dominate conservative policymaking for five decades. Under his guidance, Heritage grows to 250 employees with $80 million in annual income, becoming the institutional core of the conservative movement's policy apparatus.
