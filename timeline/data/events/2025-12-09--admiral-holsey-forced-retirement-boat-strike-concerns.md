Admiral Alvin Holsey, commander of U.S. Southern Command overseeing Caribbean operations, was forced to retire two years early after clashing with Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth over the legality of boat strikes targeting suspected drug traffickers.

Holsey had expressed concerns about the "murky" legality of the operations, particularly after a September 2, 2025 incident where the military deliberately killed two survivors of an initial boat strike who were clinging to wreckage. On December 9, Holsey privately briefed congressional lawmakers for the first time since revelations about the September 2 incident. He officially retired December 12 in a ceremony transferring command to Lt. Gen. Evan Pettus.

Senate Armed Services ranking member Jack Reed called the departure "troubling" given escalating tensions with Venezuela. The forced retirement sends a clear message to military commanders that questioning the legality or ethics of administration operations will end careers.

The incident exemplifies how the administration punishes officials who raise legal or ethical concerns, replacing experienced commanders with those willing to execute questionable orders without objection. It represents the subordination of military professionalism and legal compliance to political loyalty.
