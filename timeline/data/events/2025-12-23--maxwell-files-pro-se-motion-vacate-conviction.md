Ghislaine Maxwell files a pro se motion to vacate her conviction, arguing that "substantial new evidence" from recently released documents proves selective prosecution. Maxwell's self-written brief highlights that four individuals named as potential co-conspirators in Jeffrey Epstein's 2008 non-prosecution agreement—including Sarah Kellen and Nadia Marcinkova—were never criminally charged despite extensive evidence of their participation in the trafficking operation.

The motion also references approximately 25 victims who allegedly signed non-disclosure agreements with wealthy Epstein associates, suggesting a broader network of complicit individuals who faced no legal consequences. Maxwell argues she was singled out for prosecution while others equally or more culpable remained protected.

While legal experts consider the motion unlikely to succeed, the filing forces into the court record documented evidence of the two-tiered justice system that has characterized the Epstein case. The 2008 NPA negotiated by then-U.S. Attorney Alexander Acosta (later Trump's Labor Secretary) explicitly granted immunity to "potential co-conspirators" whose identities remain partially obscured.

Maxwell is currently serving a 20-year sentence at FCI Tallahassee for sex trafficking of minors.
