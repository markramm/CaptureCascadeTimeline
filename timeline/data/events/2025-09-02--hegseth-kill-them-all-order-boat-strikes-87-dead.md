Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth allegedly issues a verbal "kill them all" order before a September 2 strike on a suspected drug boat in the Caribbean. The first strike kills 9 of 11 people aboard. Approximately 40 minutes later, a second strike is ordered to kill the two survivors clinging to wreckage. In total, at least 87 people have been killed in 22 vessel strikes as part of the Trump administration's Caribbean drug interdiction campaign.

Rep. Adam Smith describes the survivors as "basically two shirtless people clinging to the bow of a capsized and inoperable boat" before missiles killed them. Congressional Democrats are investigating whether the attacks constitute war crimes. Hegseth denies giving a specific "kill order" but states he "fully support[s] that strike."

Stephen Miller, as architect of the administration's border and drug policies, reportedly pushed for military strikes against cartels. The Washington Post reports Miller and other senior officials "were looking for a fight" and his "hard-line Mexico strategy morphed into deadly boat strikes."

Lawmakers have not specifically authorized military force against alleged drug boats. The Pentagon refuses to release video footage of the strikes. The campaign represents an unprecedented use of military force in drug interdiction without congressional authorization, potentially violating both domestic law and international humanitarian law regarding the treatment of shipwreck survivors.
