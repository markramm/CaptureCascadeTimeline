A July 2019 FBI internal email released as part of the ongoing Epstein document disclosures reveals the agency was tracking "10 co-conspirators" in the Epstein sex trafficking investigation. Seven of the ten names are redacted in the released version, raising questions about which powerful figures remain shielded from public accountability.

One of the redacted individuals is described as a "wealthy business man in Ohio," widely understood to reference Les Wexner, the billionaire founder of L Brands (Victoria's Secret, Bath & Body Works) who had extensive documented financial ties to Epstein. Wexner granted Epstein power of attorney over his affairs and later claimed Epstein "misappropriated" funds, though criminal charges were never filed.

The email asked about the "status" of these co-conspirators, suggesting active consideration of charges that were never brought. The three visible names include known Epstein associates Sarah Kellen, Nadia Marcinkova, and others named in the 2008 non-prosecution agreement but never criminally charged.

The heavy redactions in a document referencing potential criminal co-conspirators exemplifies the pattern of elite protection that has characterized the Epstein case from Alexander Acosta's 2008 sweetheart deal through the present.
