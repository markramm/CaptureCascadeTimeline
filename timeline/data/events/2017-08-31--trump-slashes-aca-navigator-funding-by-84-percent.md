The Trump administration's Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services announced devastating cuts to the ACA's consumer assistance programs: navigator funding slashed from $62.5 million (2017) to $10 million (2018)—an 84% reduction—while advertising and outreach dropped from $100 million to $10 million, a 90% cut. Navigator programs provide critical enrollment assistance, helping consumers understand coverage options, verify income for subsidies, and reach underserved minority communities. The organizations employ trained counselors who help people with complex enrollment processes, billing issues, and ACA compliance. CMS Administrator Seema Verma claimed the cuts reflected 'performance-based' funding, but surveys documented massive unmet need for enrollment help, with consumers citing difficulty understanding options and lacking knowledge about enrollment. Research conclusively showed federally funded advertising was more effective than private ads at driving marketplace enrollment, and the gutting of navigators did not spur compensating private outreach. ACA enrollment declined by 2.5 million over Trump's first term. Subsequent studies attributed the enrollment drop directly to reduced navigator support and outreach. This sabotage disproportionately harmed vulnerable populations navigating complex insurance markets. The cuts represented administrative warfare on legislation when repeal failed legislatively. In February 2025, the second Trump administration repeated these cuts, again reducing navigator funding from $98 million to $10 million annually—demonstrating systematic commitment to undermining ACA enrollment infrastructure.
