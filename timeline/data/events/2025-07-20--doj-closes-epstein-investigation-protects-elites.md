The Department of Justice announced it was closing its investigation into the Epstein case, declaring there was no evidence of an unreleased "client list" and no basis for murder charges. The announcement came after weeks of pressure from Trump's base demanding full transparency about Epstein's associates.

AG Pam Bondi stated that DOJ had reviewed all available evidence and found nothing warranting further prosecution or document release. The closure came despite widespread skepticism about the thoroughness of the investigation and known gaps in publicly released materials.

Trump had repeatedly promised during the 2024 campaign to release "everything" about Epstein, but the DOJ closure effectively ended that possibility. Critics noted the closure protected potentially implicated elites—including those with connections to the administration—from further scrutiny.

The announcement represented a stark reversal from campaign rhetoric, with Trump's DOJ effectively becoming the protector of Epstein-related secrets rather than their exposer. Legal observers noted that several known associates of Epstein remained unindicted despite substantial evidence of their involvement in his trafficking operation.
