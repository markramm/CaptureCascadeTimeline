The Department of Justice announces it has discovered over one million additional documents potentially related to Jeffrey Epstein, even as the agency has released only a small fraction of the materials already in its possession. The revelation comes amid growing bipartisan Congressional frustration with the pace and scope of document releases.

Representatives Thomas Massie (R-KY) and Ro Khanna (D-CA) threaten contempt proceedings against DOJ officials for the slow-walking of document production. The bipartisan pressure reflects rare agreement that the Epstein case involves systemic failures that transcend partisan politics.

The timing raises questions about DOJ's document management and whether the discovery of additional materials represents genuine new findings or an attempt to further delay meaningful disclosure. Critics note that years after Epstein's death and Maxwell's conviction, the full scope of his trafficking network and its elite clientele remains obscured by redactions and delayed releases.
