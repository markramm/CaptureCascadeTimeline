The Trump administration releases its National Security Strategy articulating a "Trump Corollary" to the Monroe Doctrine, fundamentally reorienting US strategic priorities away from Europe and toward the Western Hemisphere.

The strategy declares the Western Hemisphere as the top US security priority and pledges to "deny non-Hemispheric competitors the ability to position forces or other threatening capabilities" in the Americas. It calls for shifting US military assets to the Western Hemisphere "away from theatres whose relative import to American national security has declined"—a clear reference to Europe.

The document identifies three hemispheric threats: migration, drugs/crime, and China. It sharply criticizes European allies for their migration and free speech policies, warning they face the "prospect of civilizational erasure." French analyst Sylvie Matelly described the Europe section as "three pages full of vitriol."

EU leader Costa acknowledged a "changed relationship" between the US and Europe following the strategy's release. The document provides intellectual justification for the administration's aggressive actions in Venezuela, military strikes in the Caribbean, and pressure on Canada, Greenland, and Panama.

The strategy also labels internal bureaucratic resistance as "subversion" linked to foreign powers, alleging "ideological infiltration" and "institutional capture" are mechanisms of "strategic psychological warfare"—providing justification for purging career civil servants as national security threats.
