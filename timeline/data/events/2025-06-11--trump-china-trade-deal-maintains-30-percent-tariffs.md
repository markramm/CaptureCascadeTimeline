On June 11, 2025, President Trump announced a trade deal with China that would maintain the current 20% "fentanyl" and 10% "reciprocal" tariffs (for a total of 30%), pausing higher tariffs for 60 days while negotiations continued. The deal represented a significant softening of Trump's trade war stance following intense corporate pressure. On April 21, 2025, CEOs of major US retailers had warned Trump that the trade war with China would lead to visible price increases and product shortages within two weeks. Polls indicated 84% of business leaders were concerned about the tariffs' impact on the US economy. In response to the corporate pressure, Trump reportedly pivoted toward negotiation, sidelining hardliner Peter Navarro and promoting Scott Bessent as his lead economic advisor. On June 10, the day before the deal was announced, the American Chamber of Commerce in Taiwan publicly urged Washington to cancel its tariffs.
