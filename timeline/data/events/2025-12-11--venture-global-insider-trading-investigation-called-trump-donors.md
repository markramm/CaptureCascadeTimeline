A Democratic senator calls for an insider trading investigation into two billionaire co-chairs of Venture Global LNG after they purchased approximately $12 million in company shares each in March 2025—just days after meeting with Trump administration officials including Energy Secretary Chris Wright. Shortly thereafter, the Department of Energy granted the company an export license essential for its European expansion plans.

Robert Pender and Michael Sabel's combined $24 million stock purchase, made with apparent knowledge of the pending regulatory decision, represents a potential securities violation. The timing suggests the executives may have had nonpublic information about the favorable DOE decision when making their purchases.

Venture Global donated $1 million to Trump's inauguration, and individuals connected to the company spent $810,000 on lobbying in 2025—a twelve-fold increase from their previous high. The pattern suggests a pay-to-play arrangement: inauguration donations and lobbying expenditures leading to regulatory approvals that enrich company executives.

The case exemplifies how the Trump administration's regulatory apparatus appears to function as a mechanism for enriching political supporters, with company executives positioned to profit from favorable decisions they help secure through political contributions and access.
