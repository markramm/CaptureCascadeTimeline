The CDC's Advisory Committee on Immunization Practices (ACIP) voted 8-3 on December 5, 2025 to recommend "individual-based decision-making" for the hepatitis B vaccine birth dose for infants born to women who test negative for the virus. The CDC officially adopted this approach on December 16, ending the universal recommendation in place since 1991.

Since the 1991 recommendation, hepatitis B infections among infants and children have dropped 99%. The American Association of Immunologists expressed "extreme disappointment," calling the change a "dangerous departure" from evidence-based medicine. The American Academy of Pediatrics continues to recommend the birth dose.

The policy change came under an HHS led by Robert F. Kennedy Jr., a longtime vaccine skeptic who has spread misinformation about vaccine safety. Critics note the decision prioritizes parental "choice" over public health protection, despite hepatitis B being a serious disease that can cause liver cancer and chronic infection.

The change exemplifies how anti-vaccine ideology has captured federal health institutions, potentially reversing decades of progress in disease prevention. Public health experts warn that weakening universal vaccination recommendations will lead to preventable infections and deaths, particularly among vulnerable newborns.
