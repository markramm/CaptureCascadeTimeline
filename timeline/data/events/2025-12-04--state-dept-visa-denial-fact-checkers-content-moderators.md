The State Department issued an internal memo instructing consular officers to reject visa applications from people who worked on fact-checking, content moderation, or activities the Trump administration considers "censorship" of Americans' speech. The directive primarily targets H-1B visas for highly skilled workers.

The memo instructs consular officers to "thoroughly explore" work histories by reviewing resumes, LinkedIn profiles, and media appearances for activities including: combatting misinformation or disinformation, fact-checking, content moderation, compliance, and trust and safety work. The State Department also announced H-1B applicants must set social media profiles to "public" for review.

Critics warn the policy conflates legitimate content moderation work—including child safety protection—with censorship. First Amendment experts called the policy unconstitutional, noting it punishes people for lawful speech and creates a chilling effect on the entire content moderation industry.

The directive effectively bars international workers in trust and safety roles from entering the United States, potentially crippling platforms' ability to combat harmful content including child exploitation, terrorism, and fraud. It represents the administration treating factual accuracy as a threat to be suppressed rather than a public good.
