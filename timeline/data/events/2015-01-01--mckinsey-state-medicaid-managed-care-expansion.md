McKinsey expands its consulting practice advising state Medicaid agencies on 'next-generation contracting' and managed care restructuring for individuals with special or supportive care needs, including those with behavioral health conditions, intellectual or developmental disabilities, and those requiring long-term services and supports. These populations constitute only 20-25% of the Medicaid population but account for 35% of national healthcare expenditures.

McKinsey promotes managed care approaches that prioritize cost containment over quality of care, advising states on how to shift financial risk to managed care organizations while reducing state spending. The firm's support to state healthcare agencies extends across program redesign, waiver development, managed care contracting and performance management, and program governance. Each year, the United States spends over $800 billion on care delivery to these vulnerable populations, with Medicaid bearing about two-thirds of these costs.

McKinsey's consulting work helps states implement 'efficiency' measures that often result in reduced access to services, tighter eligibility rules, and lower reimbursement rates for healthcare providers serving Medicaid patients. The firm's recommendations focus on 'value-based payment models' and 'financial sustainability' - frameworks that can mask cuts to services that vulnerable populations depend on for survival.

This consulting work exemplifies how McKinsey profits from advising governments on healthcare restructuring that harms marginalized populations. While the firm promotes its work as improving care delivery and outcomes, the primary driver is cost reduction in a context where Medicaid already provides inadequate reimbursement rates that many providers refuse to accept. McKinsey's recommendations help states implement managed care systems that create additional barriers between patients and care while extracting profits for managed care corporations.
