President Trump signed a proclamation requiring a $100,000 fee for new H-1B visa petitions, representing a 2,000-5,000% increase from previous fees. The policy, effective September 21, would affect over 70% Indian visa holders and major tech companies including Amazon (10,000+ H-1B holders), Microsoft, and Meta (5,000+ each).

Economists warned the fee would trigger a "brain drain" as highly skilled workers seek opportunities in Canada, Europe, or Asia rather than paying the prohibitive fee. The policy contradicts tech industry arguments that H-1B workers fill critical skills gaps that cannot be met domestically.

The dramatic fee increase satisfied anti-immigration advocates who had long targeted the H-1B program, while creating a new revenue stream for the federal government. Critics noted the policy would harm American competitiveness in technology and research sectors where immigrant talent has historically driven innovation.

The same-day announcement of the "Gold Card" million-dollar citizenship program created a stark two-tier system: wealthy foreigners could purchase residency while skilled workers faced prohibitive barriers. The contrast highlighted how immigration policy was being reshaped to favor wealth over talent.
