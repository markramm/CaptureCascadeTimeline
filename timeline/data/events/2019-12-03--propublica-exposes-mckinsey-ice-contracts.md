ProPublica publishes a major investigation revealing McKinsey & Company's work for ICE ultimately totaled more than $20 million in consulting fees, with the firm recommending cuts to spending on food, medical care, and supervision of immigration detainees. The investigation is based on interviews with people who worked on the project for both ICE and McKinsey, plus 1,500 pages of documents obtained from the agency after ProPublica filed a lawsuit under the Freedom of Information Act. The reporting reveals McKinsey had such extensive sway within the agency that the firm ghostwrote a government document outlining their own responsibilities and justifying their $2.2 million contract extension. While McKinsey's ICE work ended in July 2018, the firm signed a contract with Customs and Border Protection one week later worth $2 million, and signed another CBP contract worth up to $8.4 million that would keep the firm with the agency until at least September 2020. The firm's global managing partner Kevin Sneader had assured employees in a 2018 email that the firm had never focused on developing, advising or implementing immigration policies, and said McKinsey 'will not, under any circumstances, engage in work, anywhere in the world, that advances or assists policies that are at odds with our values.' Representative Pramila Jayapal, Vice Chair of the House Judiciary Committee on Immigration and Citizenship, writes to McKinsey requesting information on its ICE work. The investigation adds to mounting evidence of McKinsey's willingness to compromise stated ethical principles for lucrative government contracts, even when recommendations endanger vulnerable populations.
