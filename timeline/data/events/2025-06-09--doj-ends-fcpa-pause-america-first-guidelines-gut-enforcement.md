The Department of Justice officially ended its 180-day pause on Foreign Corrupt Practices Act enforcement by issuing new "America First" guidelines that fundamentally gut anti-bribery enforcement. The new policy deprioritizes prosecution of US companies bribing foreign officials and redirects resources toward cases supposedly affecting American economic competitiveness.

Under the new framework, DOJ will consider whether FCPA enforcement would disadvantage American companies relative to foreign competitors—effectively legitimizing bribery if other countries allow it. The guidelines also raise prosecution thresholds and require additional approval layers for bringing cases.

AG Pam Bondi framed the changes as "refocusing" enforcement, but critics noted the guidelines make it nearly impossible to prosecute corporate foreign bribery. The policy reverses decades of bipartisan anti-corruption consensus that positioned the US as a global leader in combating bribery.

The changes benefit Trump-connected companies with extensive international operations, particularly in sectors like oil and gas, defense, and pharmaceuticals where foreign bribery has historically been common. Coming after the February 10 enforcement pause that allowed cases to lapse, the new policy completes the administration's dismantling of America's anti-corruption infrastructure.
