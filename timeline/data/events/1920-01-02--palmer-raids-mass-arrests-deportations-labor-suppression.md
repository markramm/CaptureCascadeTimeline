Attorney General A. Mitchell Palmer and Bureau of Investigation chief J. Edgar Hoover orchestrated simultaneous raids across 36 cities, arresting over 6,000 people without warrants under the Immigration Act of 1918. The raids specifically targeted labor union leaders, particularly the Industrial Workers of the World, along with Italian anarchists and Eastern European Jewish immigrants with alleged leftist ties. Of 10,000 arrested during the Palmer Raids overall, 3,500 were held in detention and 556 resident aliens were eventually deported. The raids established mass surveillance and deportation as tools for labor suppression, with Acting Labor Secretary Louis Freeland Post later canceling over 2,000 warrants as illegal. The nascent ACLU published documentation of systematic Justice Department violations of civil liberties, but the precedent for weaponizing immigration enforcement against labor organizing was established.
