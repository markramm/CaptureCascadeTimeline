William J. Baroody Sr. quits his position at the U.S. Chamber of Commerce to join the American Enterprise Association as Executive Director after A.D. Marshall, head of General Electric, assumes the institution's presidency and hires Baroody along with W. Glenn Campbell to head the research program. Baroody's arrival marks the beginning of AEI's transformation from a languishing business organization into one of the great conservative forces in Washington, with young Republican congressman Melvin Laird serving as his friend and adviser. Baroody raises money to expand AEA's financial base beyond business leaders on the board, securing funding primarily from the Howard Pew Freedom Trust, with additional support from the Lilly Endowment, Scaife Fund, Earhart Foundation, Kresge Foundation, and major corporate sponsors. During the 1950s and 1960s under Baroody's leadership, AEA's work becomes more pointed and focused, publishing monographs by Edward Banfield, James M. Buchanan, P.T. Bauer, Alfred de Grazia, Rose Friedman, and Gottfried Haberler while significantly improving research rigorousness and diversifying fundraising. The organization changes its name to American Enterprise Institute for Public Policy Research in 1962 to avoid confusion with a trade association, with Baroody assuming the presidency and setting about reconfiguring the institution's operations. This 1954 expansion precedes the Powell Memo by 17 years, demonstrating that corporate-funded conservative think tank infrastructure was being systematically built well before Powell's 1971 blueprint called for business to establish "staff of scholars" to develop positions and arguments. Baroody's AEI becomes the template for the Heritage Foundation, Cato Institute, and other Powell Memo-inspired institutions.
