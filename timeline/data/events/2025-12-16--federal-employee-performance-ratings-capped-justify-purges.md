Trump administration officials began implementing strict quotas on federal employee performance ratings despite such "forced distribution" systems being explicitly prohibited under current federal regulations (5 CFR 430.208). The National Park Service instructed superintendents that no more than 5% of employees could receive "outstanding" ratings and no more than 25% could receive "exceeds expectations."

OPM Director Scott Kupor circulated a draft regulatory proposal to remove the prohibition on forced distribution and extend a 30% cap on top ratings across the entire civil service of more than 2 million employees. Kupor claimed "ratings inflation is so deeply embedded in the performance management culture" that changes are needed.

Federal employees and legal experts warned the policy violates federal law requiring ratings be based solely on actual job performance, not predetermined quotas. The system is designed to artificially create categories of lower-rated employees who can then be targeted for "reduction in force" layoffs. Park superintendents who resisted the illegal policy were reportedly threatened with insubordination charges.

The policy represents an attempt to purge career civil servants by manufacturing poor performance records rather than demonstrating actual performance deficiencies—circumventing civil service protections through administrative manipulation.
