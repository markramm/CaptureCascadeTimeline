USCIS issued internal guidance directing field offices to supply the Office of Immigration Litigation with 100-200 denaturalization case referrals per month during fiscal year 2026. This represents a twentyfold increase from historical averages—between 2017-2025, DOJ filed only 120 total denaturalization cases.

The quota could result in 2,400 annual referrals, potentially exceeding the previous eight-year total in a single month. The directive applies to approximately 26 million naturalized U.S. citizens, creating unprecedented uncertainty for immigrants who became Americans through the legal process.

Former USCIS officials criticized the quota system as potentially politicizing citizenship revocation and turning "a serious and rare tool into a blunt instrument." Denaturalization has historically been reserved for cases of fraud in the naturalization process or participation in Nazi war crimes, not deployed as a mass enforcement mechanism.

The policy transforms citizenship from a permanent status into a conditional one subject to administrative quotas. Critics warn it creates a two-tiered citizenship system where naturalized citizens face perpetual scrutiny that native-born citizens do not, chilling legal immigration and punishing immigrants who followed the rules.
