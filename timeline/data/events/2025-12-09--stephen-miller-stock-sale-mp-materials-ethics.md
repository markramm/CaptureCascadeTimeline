According to reports published December 9, 2025, Stephen Miller, Trump's deputy chief of staff, sold $50,000-$100,000 in MP Materials stock on August 14, 2025—one month after the Trump administration announced a major deal benefiting the company.

On July 10, 2025, the administration announced a $400 million equity investment (15% stake) plus a $150 million Defense Department loan to MP Materials, the only rare earth mining and processing company in the United States. The government also committed to a 10-year minimum price guarantee and to purchase all magnets from a planned facility.

MP Materials stock rose from $30.03 on July 9 to $76.58 on August 14—a historic high—when Miller sold his shares. Ethics experts noted the sale appeared legal under current rules but called it "a really bad look" given the timing and Miller's senior policy role in the administration.

The transaction exemplifies how administration officials can profit from policy decisions they influence, even when technically complying with ethics rules. The pattern of officials benefiting financially from government actions they help shape undermines public trust in impartial governance.
