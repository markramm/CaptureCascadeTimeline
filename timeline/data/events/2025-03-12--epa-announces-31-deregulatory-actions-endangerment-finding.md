EPA Administrator Lee Zeldin announced 31 deregulatory actions targeting foundational environmental protections, including the 2009 Endangerment Finding that legally establishes greenhouse gases as threats to public health. Zeldin described the effort as driving "a dagger through the heart of the climate-change religion."

The Endangerment Finding, based on decades of climate science, provides the legal foundation for all EPA carbon regulations. Overturning it would eliminate the agency's authority to regulate greenhouse gas emissions and undo climate policies across the federal government.

Other targeted regulations include the Clean Power Plan, vehicle emission standards, methane rules for oil and gas operations, and chemical safety requirements. The scope of the rollback exceeds any previous deregulatory effort in EPA history.

Zeldin's explicitly ideological framing—describing climate science as "religion" to be attacked—revealed that the deregulation is driven by political opposition to climate action rather than scientific or economic analysis. Environmental and public health groups estimated the combined rollbacks could lead to tens of thousands of additional premature deaths and billions in health costs annually.
