President Trump issued a "full and unconditional pardon" for Oak View Group co-founder Tim Leiweke, who had been indicted just months earlier in July 2025 by Trump's own Justice Department for conspiracy to rig the bidding process for the $375 million Moody Center arena at the University of Texas at Austin.

The charge carried a maximum penalty of 10 years in prison and a $1 million fine. Leiweke had pleaded not guilty and was represented by Trump ally and former Rep. Trey Gowdy. Oak View Group separately agreed to pay $15 million in penalties over the bid-rigging claims.

Ironically, Leiweke had previously criticized Trump as the world's "single greatest Con man" in since-deleted tweets—demonstrating that past criticism of Trump poses no barrier to receiving pardons when connected to the right lawyers and willing to pay penalties.

The pardon undermines Trump's own DOJ prosecution and signals that white-collar defendants with resources and political connections can escape accountability regardless of the strength of evidence against them. It exemplifies the two-tiered justice system where corporate executives receive clemency while ordinary defendants face the full weight of prosecution.
