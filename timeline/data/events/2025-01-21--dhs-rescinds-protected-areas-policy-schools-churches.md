On Trump's first full day in office, Acting DHS Secretary Benjamine Huffman rescinded the "Protected Areas" policy that had prohibited immigration enforcement at sensitive locations including schools, daycare centers, playgrounds, school bus stops, churches, and hospitals. The original sensitive locations policy dated to 2011 under the Obama administration.

The rescission immediately enabled ICE arrests at locations previously considered off-limits. Documented incidents include a Chicago daycare raid where armed agents detained a childcare worker while children watched, arrests of parents in school parking lots during drop-off/pick-up, and a San Diego father detained near an elementary school while driving his daughter to class.

Nearly 2,000 children were arrested by ICE between January 20 and July 31, 2025. Research documented unusual increases in student absences across southern California schools coinciding with concentrated immigration enforcement operations, as families kept children home fearing arrest.

The policy change traumatizes children witnessing armed raids, undermines access to education as families fear schools, and separates families at locations that were designed to be safe spaces. Approximately 4.4 million children live with undocumented parents and are affected by enforcement that now reaches into their schools and places of worship.
