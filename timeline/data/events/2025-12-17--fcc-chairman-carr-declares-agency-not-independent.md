During Senate Commerce Committee testimony on December 17, FCC Chairman Brendan Carr stated the FCC "is not formally an independent agency" because commissioners can be removed by the president. Within approximately 20 minutes—while Carr was still testifying—the FCC website removed the word "independent" from its mission statement, changing "An independent U.S. government agency overseen by Congress" to simply "A U.S. government agency overseen by Congress."

The FCC's sole Democratic commissioner, Anna Gomez, rejected Carr's interpretation, stating Congress deliberately structured the FCC as independent to shield broadcast regulation from political pressure. Even Republican Senator Ted Cruz called Carr's position "dangerous as hell," warning against government "arbitrating truth or opinion."

Carr had previously threatened media companies including ABC/Disney following Jimmy Kimmel's criticism of Trump, raising concerns that the FCC is being weaponized against press freedom. The real-time website modification during testimony demonstrated coordinated messaging to eliminate the concept of agency independence.

The incident represents a broader administration effort to assert presidential control over independent regulatory agencies, undermining the separation between political pressure and technical regulatory decisions that has protected telecommunications policy for decades.
