Just one week after global managing partner Kevin Sneader announces that McKinsey's ICE engagement has ended and the firm 'will not, under any circumstances, engage in work at odds with our values,' McKinsey signs a $2 million contract to advise Customs and Border Protection (CBP) as it drafts a new border strategy. The CBP contract reveals the hollowness of McKinsey's ethical commitments: the firm simply shifts immigration enforcement consulting from one controversial agency to its sister agency.

CBP is the parent agency of the Border Patrol and operates detention facilities at the border where family separations occurred. During this period, CBP is implementing Trump's 'zero-tolerance' immigration policies and managing the fallout from the family separation crisis that generated national outrage just weeks earlier. McKinsey's willingness to immediately contract with CBP while claiming to avoid immigration policy work demonstrates corporate ethics as public relations rather than genuine principle.

Reporting reveals that one slide in McKinsey's pitch deck to CBP simply reads 'Wall'—a reference to Trump's signature border wall campaign promise. This suggests McKinsey is not providing neutral management consulting but directly supporting Trump's immigration agenda through policy development and strategic planning for border enforcement infrastructure.

McKinsey also signs another contract with CBP worth up to $8.4 million that would keep the firm working with the agency until at least September 2020. The total CBP contracts represent more than $10 million in continued immigration enforcement work, demonstrating that McKinsey's July 9 commitment to avoid policies 'at odds with our values' lasted exactly one week before the firm found a way to continue lucrative government contracts in the same policy area through a different agency.
