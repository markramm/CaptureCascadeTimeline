UPS announced during its Q1 2025 earnings release plans to cut approximately 20,000 positions—4% of its 490,000-person workforce—and close 73 facilities by June 2025. The company cited its decision to reduce Amazon shipments by 50% by late 2026, alongside "tariff uncertainty" affecting trade volumes.

UPS CFO Bryan Dykes stated the "macro environment is highly uncertain due to changing trade policy and tariff uncertainty." The cuts are expected to save $3.5 billion in 2025, with plans to automate 400 facilities to reduce labor dependency.

The layoffs represent one of the largest corporate workforce reductions of 2025, directly tied to Trump administration trade policies. As a logistics company dependent on international trade, UPS serves as a bellwether for how tariff policies ripple through the economy.

The combination of reduced Amazon business and trade uncertainty highlights how policy instability affects corporate planning. Companies cannot make long-term investments when tariff rates change unpredictably, leading to defensive layoffs and facility closures rather than growth.
