The Supreme Court issued an unsigned shadow docket order allowing the Trump administration to deport migrants to third countries—nations other than their country of origin—without meaningful judicial review. The ruling lifted a lower court injunction that had blocked the practice as a violation of due process.

The policy allows DHS to send deportees to any country willing to accept them, regardless of safety conditions or the deportee's connection to that country. Critics warned this enables deportation to dangerous locations where individuals may face persecution, essentially using third countries as dumping grounds.

The shadow docket decision came without full briefing, oral argument, or written opinions explaining the court's reasoning—exemplifying the court's increasing willingness to grant emergency relief to the Trump administration while denying similar requests from other parties.

Immigration advocates noted the ruling effectively ends asylum protections for many migrants, since they can be removed to countries where they have no legal status or family connections before their cases are heard. The decision represents another instance of the court's conservative majority enabling Trump's most aggressive immigration policies through procedural shortcuts.
