John M. Olin, president of Olin Industries' chemical and munitions manufacturing businesses, establishes the John M. Olin Foundation as a grant-making foundation, creating infrastructure that will later become one of the most influential conservative philanthropy vehicles. The foundation remains largely dormant as a conventional rich man's philanthropy supporting Cornell University Alumni Fund and other causes during its first 15 years. From 1958-1966, the foundation serves as a vehicle to launder money for the Central Intelligence Agency, which uses the funds for covert anti-communist propaganda operations, demonstrating early connections between corporate philanthropy, intelligence agencies, and ideological warfare. Olin's foundation represents patient capital for conservative movement-building, establishing the legal and financial structure that will be activated in 1968-1969 when Olin decides at age 80 to "preserve the free-market system" after being disturbed by leftist activism at Cornell. The foundation's eventual deployment of over $370 million to conservative think tanks, media outlets, and law programs at influential universities makes it the most notable funder of the law and economics movement and the Federalist Society. The 1953 founding precedes the Powell Memo by 18 years, demonstrating that corporate elites were building philanthropic infrastructure for ideological capture well before Powell's 1971 blueprint, with the foundation's CIA laundering revealing deep connections between corporate power, conservative ideology, and national security apparatus.
