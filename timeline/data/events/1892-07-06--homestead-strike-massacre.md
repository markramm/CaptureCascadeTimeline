Andrew Carnegie and manager Henry Clay Frick deployed 300 Pinkerton agents against striking steelworkers at Carnegie Steel's Homestead, Pennsylvania plant, resulting in a twelve-hour battle that killed 7-10 workers and 3-7 Pinkertons (sources vary on exact casualties, with some reporting up to 16 total deaths). Carnegie, vacationing in Scotland, had given Frick "carte blanche to break the union" before the July 1st contract expiration. On July 2, Frick fired all 3,800 workers. The Pinkertons arrived by barge at 10:30 PM on July 5th, leading to violent confrontation on July 6th with "nearly the entire town" of workers. An additional 40 workers were wounded. Four days later, 8,500 National Guard troops occupied the town at Frick's request. The crushing of the strike destroyed union organizing in steel for 26 years until 1918. Public outrage over Pinkerton violence prompted Congress to pass the Anti-Pinkerton Act in 1893, prohibiting federal hiring of private security forces for strikebreaking.
