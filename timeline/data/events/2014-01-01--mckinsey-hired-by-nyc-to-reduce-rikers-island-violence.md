New York City hires McKinsey & Company to address an alarming rise in violence at Rikers Island following media reports in early 2014 revealing that in just two years, the most serious inmate violence and use of force by guards had both jumped more than 50%. Mayor Bill de Blasio, just months into his first term, faces a groundswell of outrage after the United States attorney's office in Manhattan begins threatening legal action to force reforms.

Despite McKinsey's lack of corrections experience, the elite consulting firm wins the contract with an initial value of $1.8 million. The firm is tasked with developing strategies to reduce violence in one of America's most notorious jail complexes, which was plagued by corruption, violence, and reliance on torturous solitary confinement. The case of Kalief Browder, who spent three years at Rikers including extended solitary confinement for allegedly stealing a backpack, had become one of the highest-profile examples of inhumanity at Rikers.

This contract marks the beginning of McKinsey's nearly three-year engagement with New York City's Department of Correction that would ultimately cost taxpayers $27.5 million. The engagement represents McKinsey's expansion into criminal justice consulting and its growing role in advising public sector agencies on sensitive operational issues where the firm has no demonstrated expertise.

The decision to hire an expensive management consulting firm with no corrections background, rather than bringing in criminal justice experts or investing in direct improvements to conditions and staffing, exemplifies the outsourcing of government accountability to private consultants who face no consequences for failed outcomes.
