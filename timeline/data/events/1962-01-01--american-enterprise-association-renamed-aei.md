The American Enterprise Association changes its name to the American Enterprise Institute for Public Policy Research as William J. Baroody Sr. assumes the presidency, rebranding the organization to avoid confusion with a trade association representing business interests while maintaining the same corporate-funded conservative policy agenda. The name change gives AEI the appearance of scholarly independence and academic credibility despite continuing to receive funding from the Howard Pew Freedom Trust, Lilly Endowment, Scaife Fund, and major corporations including General Electric. Baroody, who joined AEI in 1954 from the U.S. Chamber of Commerce, uses the presidency to reconfigure the institution's operations, significantly improve research rigorousness, and diversify fundraising while publishing work by conservative scholars that challenges government regulation and promotes free-market solutions. The rebranding proves successful in establishing AEI as a respected "think tank" rather than corporate lobbying operation, allowing it to expand influence in Washington policy debates while obscuring corporate funding sources and business interests driving its agenda. This 1962 rebranding precedes the Powell Memo by 9 years but demonstrates sophisticated understanding that corporate political influence requires appearance of scholarly objectivity rather than transparent business advocacy. The "Institute" branding provides cover for what remains a corporate-funded operation working to shape policy in business interests, establishing the model for conservative think tanks that would proliferate after Powell's 1971 call for business to establish "staff of scholars" to develop positions and arguments. AEI's transformation from trade association to prestigious think tank shows corporate interests already understood by 1962 how to launder political advocacy through academic-sounding institutions.
