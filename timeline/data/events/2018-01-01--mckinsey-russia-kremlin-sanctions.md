McKinsey & Company works extensively inside Russia with Kremlin-linked companies that have been placed under sanctions by Western governments - companies that the firm helped build up over the years and, in some cases, continues to advise even after sanctions are imposed. The consulting firm has advised clients in many sectors of the Russian economy, including mining, manufacturing, oil and gas, banking, transportation and agriculture. A McKinsey official sits on the Russian government's energy board, and former McKinsey consultants have gone to work in the Russian companies they once advised, exemplifying the revolving door between the firm and Russian state-linked enterprises. In August 2018, VEB Bank - which is wholly owned by the Russian state, intertwined with Russian intelligence agencies, and under United States sanctions - hires McKinsey to develop its business strategy. The larger question raised by McKinsey's Russia work is whether the company, in pursuing legitimate business opportunities abroad, is helping to shore up President Vladimir Putin's autocratic leadership and strengthen the capacity of Russian state enterprises that operate as extensions of Kremlin power. McKinsey's work in Russia occurs simultaneously with the firm's hundreds of millions in U.S. government contracts, creating conflicts of interest where the firm advises both a strategic adversary of the United States and the U.S. government itself. McKinsey defends its global work by claiming it will not accept jobs at odds with company values and that 'change is best achieved from the inside' - though critics note this rationale has been used to justify work with virtually every authoritarian regime.
