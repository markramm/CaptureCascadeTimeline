Over 200 former DOJ employees sign a letter stating the Civil Rights Division has been "destroyed" and its mission turned "upside down" under the Trump administration. The letter coincides with the administration's formal elimination of the "disparate impact" test in civil rights enforcement—a decades-old legal standard used to prove discrimination in housing, lending, employment, education, and healthcare.

The disparate impact doctrine, established through decades of case law and regulation, allows plaintiffs to prove discrimination by showing policies have discriminatory effects even without proof of discriminatory intent. Its elimination makes it vastly harder to challenge systemic discrimination.

The rollback follows an April 2025 executive order directing agencies to eliminate disparate impact standards "to the maximum degree" across all sectors. Assistant Attorney General for Civil Rights Harmeet Dhillon oversaw the implementation, which effectively guts enforcement tools that have been central to civil rights law since the 1960s.

The Civil Rights Division has experienced a 75% attorney exodus under Dhillon's leadership. Reuters reported that two-thirds of the DOJ unit charged with defending administration policies in court have quit, with lawyers stating they could not defend policies that were "not legally justifiable." The combination of policy destruction and personnel exodus represents a systematic dismantling of federal civil rights enforcement capacity.
