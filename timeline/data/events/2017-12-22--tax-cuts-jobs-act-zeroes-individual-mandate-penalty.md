President Trump signed the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act, which included a provision eliminating the Affordable Care Act's individual mandate penalty starting in 2019 by reducing it to $0. After Republicans failed to repeal the ACA through multiple 2017 legislative attempts, they celebrated inserting mandate repeal into the tax bill as a backdoor victory. The individual mandate—requiring people to carry health insurance or pay a penalty—was designed to balance the ACA's guarantee of coverage for pre-existing conditions by ensuring healthy people enter the insurance pool, stabilizing premiums. The Congressional Budget Office estimated repeal would cause 13 million fewer Americans to have insurance by 2027 (4 million by 2019) and increase premiums by 10% in most years. Coverage losses would include 5 million from Medicaid, 5 million from nongroup markets, and 2 million from employer coverage. The CBO projected mandate repeal would reduce federal expenditures by $318 billion over 10 years—offsetting 40% of corporate tax cut costs—by reducing subsidy payments as people dropped coverage. Research using nationally representative data found mandate repeal associated with a 24% increase in the year-over-year probability of becoming newly uninsured among lower-income adults. The provision didn't technically repeal the mandate itself but zeroed penalties, creating a legally intact but unenforced requirement. This sabotage demonstrated how Republicans, unable to repeal the ACA directly, could systematically undermine it through tangential legislation, extracting coverage from millions to finance tax cuts for corporations and the wealthy.
