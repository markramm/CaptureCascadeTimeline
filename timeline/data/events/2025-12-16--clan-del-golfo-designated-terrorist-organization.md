Secretary of State Marco Rubio designated Colombia's Clan del Golfo (also known as Gaitanist Army of Colombia/AGC) as both a Foreign Terrorist Organization and Specially Designated Global Terrorist. The group has an estimated 9,000 members and evolved from right-wing paramilitary squads.

This is the 10th gang or cartel the Trump administration designated as a terrorist organization since February 2025. The designation makes it a federal crime to provide material support and enables terrorism-related charges, opening the door to potential U.S. military action in Colombia.

The designation came amid ongoing peace talks between Clan del Golfo and the Colombian government mediated by Qatar. Experts warn the U.S. action could undermine Colombian efforts to negotiate a peaceful resolution and risks escalating conflict rather than reducing it.

The terrorist designation provides legal justification for military operations in an allied country without congressional authorization for war. Combined with the administration's aggressive posture toward Venezuela and boat strikes across the Caribbean and Pacific, the Colombia designation expands potential U.S. military intervention throughout Latin America.
