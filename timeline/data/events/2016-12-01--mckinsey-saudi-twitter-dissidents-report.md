McKinsey & Company prepares a nine-page PowerPoint presentation analyzing Twitter sentiment about Saudi Arabia's 2015 economic austerity measures, identifying three influential critics by name: Khalid al-Alkami (a writer), Omar Abdulaziz (a Montreal-based Saudi activist with 182,650 followers), and an anonymous user named 'Ahmad.' The report characterizes Abdulaziz as a 'Saudi influencer with high following' with 'a multitude of negative tweets on topics such as austerity and the royal decrees.' McKinsey claims the report was prepared for internal use by an analyst in Saudi Arabia using publicly available information, not commissioned by the Saudi government. However, the document's detailed identification of regime critics raises immediate questions about how it might be used by authoritarian clients. Within months of the report's creation, all three identified dissidents face severe consequences: al-Alkami is arrested, the Ahmad account disappears, and Abdulaziz has his phone hacked and family members detained and tortured. The report exemplifies how Western consulting firms' 'neutral' analytical work can be weaponized by authoritarian regimes to identify and suppress dissent. At the time McKinsey completes this report, the firm has 137 projects ongoing in Saudi Arabia while also carrying out 64 contracts for the U.S. government, demonstrating the fundamental conflict of interest in serving both democratic and authoritarian clients simultaneously.
