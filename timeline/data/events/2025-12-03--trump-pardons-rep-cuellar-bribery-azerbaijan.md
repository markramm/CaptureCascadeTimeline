President Trump pardoned Texas Democratic Rep. Henry Cuellar and his wife Imelda in a federal bribery and conspiracy case. The couple had been indicted in 2024 on 12 counts including bribery, conspiracy, and money laundering for allegedly accepting nearly $600,000 in bribes from Azerbaijan's state oil company and a Mexican commercial bank.

The indictment alleged money was laundered through shell companies owned by Imelda Cuellar, and that the congressman pushed policy benefiting Azerbaijani interests including delivering a pro-Azerbaijan speech on the House floor. Despite being a Democrat, Cuellar was one of Congress's most conservative Democrats who frequently criticized Biden's immigration policies.

Trump claimed without evidence that Cuellar was prosecuted for criticizing Biden's immigration policies—a false narrative contradicted by the detailed evidence of foreign bribery. Cuellar still faces a House Ethics Committee investigation that began in May 2024.

The pardon demonstrates that Trump's clemency extends across party lines when it serves his narrative—in this case, using a Democratic corruption case to falsely suggest Biden's DOJ engaged in political prosecution, while simultaneously normalizing acceptance of foreign bribes by elected officials.
