The IMF Executive Board approves the largest stand-by arrangement in the Fund's history—$57.1 billion (1,227% of Argentina's IMF quota) to support President Mauricio Macri's 2018-21 economic program. After an augmentation in October 2018, the total package reaches $57 billion to be disbursed over three years. The bailout responds to renewed economic pressures, capital flight, and rapidly depreciating peso following 'turbulence' in April 2018 when the peso devalued 11% against the dollar in less than a week amid rampant inflation, recession, high unemployment, and wider emerging market concerns. In June, Macri's government signs the initial $50 billion deal; on September 26, Economy Minister Nicolás Dujovne announces the IMF increases the loan by $7.1 billion. The stated purpose is stemming capital outflow, supporting the falling peso, and encouraging foreign investment. The program catastrophically fails: only four of twelve planned reviews are completed, and it does not achieve objectives of restoring confidence in fiscal and external viability while fostering growth. The arrangement is canceled on July 24, 2020. Only $44 billion is ultimately disbursed after Macri's successor, Alberto Fernández, refuses to accept the remainder and seeks to renegotiate repayment terms. The failed bailout leaves Argentina owing more to the IMF than any other nation, demonstrating a recurring pattern: Argentina's 2001 historic default resulted from IMF austerity programs, yet seventeen years later the country returns to the Fund for an even larger loan that again fails. The cycle illustrates how IMF programs trap countries in recurring debt crises—extracting wealth through interest payments and austerity conditions while never actually resolving underlying economic problems, instead creating permanent dependency that enriches international creditors while impoverishing local populations. Argentina's experience bookends two decades of failed neoliberal orthodoxy, from the largest default in 2001 to the largest bailout in 2018, both ending in economic devastation.
