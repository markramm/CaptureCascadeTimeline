President Trump grants a full pardon to former Honduran President Juan Orlando Hernández, who had been convicted in U.S. federal court of conspiring to traffic more than 400 tons of cocaine into the United States and sentenced to 45 years in prison. Hernández received bribes from drug traffickers including Joaquín "El Chapo" Guzmán of the Sinaloa Cartel.

Trump claimed without evidence that the prosecution was a "Biden setup." The pardon was secured through a letter praising Trump and lobbying by Roger Stone, the longtime Trump advisor previously pardoned by Trump in his first term.

Bipartisan lawmakers criticized the pardon as contradicting U.S. anti-drug trafficking efforts. Honduras sought to arrest Hernández upon his release to face domestic charges. The DEA and federal prosecutors had worked for years to build the case against Hernández, gathering evidence of his protection of cocaine shipments and receipt of millions in cartel bribes.

A House Judiciary Committee report found Trump's second-term pardons have wiped out over $1.3 billion in restitution and fines, undermining accountability to victims. Of approximately 10,000 pardon petitions filed in 2025, only 10 ordinary people have been granted clemency—demonstrating the pardon power is being used for political allies and those with lobbying connections, not justice.
