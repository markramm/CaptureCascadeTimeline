On December 9, 2025, President Trump claimed without evidence that all four Biden Federal Reserve appointments may have been signed by autopen rather than personally, suggesting they could be invalid. Trump asked Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent to investigate, saying "Would you check that?"

The targeted governors potentially include Fed Chair Jerome Powell, Vice Chair Philip Jefferson, Governor Michael Barr, and Governor Lisa Cook. Trump has repeatedly criticized Powell and the Fed's interest rate decisions, and removing Biden appointees would allow Trump to reshape the central bank.

Legal experts dismissed the gambit as unlikely to succeed, noting governors would mount legal challenges to any invalidation attempt. The claim follows broader Trump administration investigations into Biden's use of autopens for executive actions.

The threat represents an escalating assault on Federal Reserve independence, a cornerstone of stable monetary policy since the Fed's creation. Central bank independence insulates interest rate decisions from political pressure; eliminating it would allow presidents to manipulate monetary policy for electoral advantage, potentially destabilizing the economy.
