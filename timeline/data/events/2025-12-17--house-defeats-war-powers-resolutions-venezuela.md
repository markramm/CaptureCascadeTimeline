The House rejected two War Powers resolutions aimed at stopping President Trump from carrying out strikes on suspected drug boats or attacking Venezuela without congressional approval. Rep. Gregory Meeks' measure to remove forces from hostilities with designated terrorist organizations in the Western Hemisphere failed 210-216. Rep. Jim McGovern's resolution on Venezuela hostilities failed 211-213.

At the time of the votes, at least 95-107 people had been killed in the boat strike campaign. Only 2-3 Republicans voted with Democrats in favor of the resolutions, while 1-2 Democrats voted against or abstained. The votes gave Trump effective congressional acquiescence to continue undeclared military operations.

The defeat of both resolutions represents congressional abdication of war powers authority. The strikes have killed over 100 people without any declaration of war, without congressional authorization for military force, and without public evidence that targets were carrying drugs.

The votes establish precedent for presidents to conduct lethal military operations against alleged drug traffickers anywhere in the hemisphere without congressional oversight. Critics warned this blank check for undeclared war undermines constitutional constraints on executive military power.
