McKinsey & Company, the elite international consulting firm, begins a multi-million dollar contract with Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) under the Obama administration to help engineer an 'organizational transformation' in the ICE division charged with deporting migrants who are in the United States unlawfully. The initial contract is designed to help the agency find ways to operate more effectively and cost-efficiently, focusing on process optimization and organizational structure.

This Obama-era contract establishes McKinsey's deep integration into ICE operations and creates the consulting relationship that would later be redirected toward Trump's immigration crackdown. The contract originated during a period when ICE faced a surge in undocumented migrants and sought management consulting expertise to handle increased deportation processing.

McKinsey's entry into immigration enforcement work represents the consulting industry's expansion into politically sensitive government operations. The firm's work on 'organizational transformation' gives McKinsey consultants access to ICE's internal operations, detention facilities, and enforcement processes, positioning the firm to influence the agency's fundamental approach to immigration enforcement.

The contract would ultimately grow to over $20 million in total value as McKinsey's work continued and expanded after Trump took office in January 2017. What began as organizational consulting under Obama would transform into optimization of Trump's deportation machine, raising questions about corporate complicity in controversial government policies and the ethical responsibilities of management consultants working on politically charged issues.
