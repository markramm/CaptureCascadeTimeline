Senator Maria Cantwell reveals that FAA Administrator Bryan Bedford violated his ethics agreement by failing to divest up to $30 million in airline stock within the required 90-day deadline. Bedford, confirmed on July 9, 2025, was required to divest his Republic Airways Holdings stock by October 7, but financial disclosures show he had not done so.

The Office of Government Ethics denied Bedford's request to amend his ethics agreement on December 9, 2025, one day before Cantwell's disclosure. The violation is particularly egregious because the Republic-Mesa Air Group merger, announced November 25, 2025, nearly doubled the value of Bedford's stock holdings while he serves as the regulator overseeing the merger and the broader aviation industry.

Bedford previously served as CEO of Republic Airways, creating a textbook regulatory capture scenario: the former airline executive now regulates his former company while maintaining a multimillion-dollar financial stake in its success. The aviation industry he is supposed to regulate can directly enrich him through stock appreciation.

This represents the broader pattern of Trump administration officials maintaining financial conflicts with the industries they oversee, enabled by the February 2025 firing of OGE Director David Huitema which eliminated independent ethics oversight.
