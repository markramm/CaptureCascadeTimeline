The first national strike in U.S. history began when Baltimore and Ohio Railroad workers in Martinsburg, West Virginia walked out against wage cuts. President Hayes deployed federal troops for the first time against striking workers, establishing a precedent for using state military power to protect corporate interests. The Pinkertons were hired to suppress strikers, killing an estimated 100 people nationwide. In Baltimore, Maryland National Guard troops killed 10 civilians and wounded 25. In Pittsburgh, 46 people died including 20 civilians (among them women and children) killed when troops fired into crowds, followed by 26 more deaths in subsequent violence. This alliance between corporate power, private security forces, and federal military intervention would become the template for Gilded Age labor suppression, with strikes broken by early August through coordinated state violence.
