summary: 'Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth authorized a large-scale military operation targeting ISIS positions across Syria on December 20, 2025, in retaliation for an ambush attack that killed two Iowa National Guard soldiers and a civilian interpreter on December 13. Operation Hawkeye Strike employed over 70 airstrikes using approximately 100 munitions, deploying F-15 and A-10 fighter jets, Apache attack helicopters, and HIMARS rocket artillery systems against ISIS weapon storage facilities, infrastructure, and operational positions in central Syria.


  Hegseth characterized the operation as "not the beginning of a war—it is a declaration of vengeance," emphasizing the Trump administration''s commitment to aggressive military responses to attacks on American personnel. The operation represented one of the most significant U.S. military actions in Syria since the formal defeat of ISIS''s territorial caliphate in 2019, signaling a shift toward more aggressive counterterrorism policies under the Trump administration''s second term.


  The strikes came days after President Trump attended a dignified transfer ceremony at Dover Air Force Base on December 17 for the fallen service members—Sgt. Edgar Brian Torres-Tovar (25), Sgt. William Nathaniel Howard (29), and civilian interpreter Ayad Mansoor Sakat. Trump had promised "very serious retaliation" while noting that Syria''s new government had provided full cooperation with U.S. forces. The operation also received support from Jordan, whose fighter aircraft participated in a supporting role.


  U.S. Central Command reported that the strikes focused on eliminating ISIS capabilities in regions where an estimated 1,500-3,000 ISIS militants remain active across Syria and Iraq. The scale and scope of the operation—involving multiple military platforms and extensive munitions deployment—demonstrated the Trump administration''s willingness to use overwhelming force in response to attacks on American personnel, marking a notable escalation in U.S. counterterrorism operations in the Middle East.


  The operation occurred amid ongoing Congressional scrutiny of Defense Secretary Hegseth over other military operations, including controversial boat strikes near Venezuela. However, Operation Hawkeye Strike received bipartisan support from lawmakers who viewed it as an appropriate response to the killing of American service members. The strikes underscored the continued U.S. military presence in Syria and the persistent threat posed by ISIS remnants despite the organization''s territorial defeat years earlier.


  Hegseth''s public framing of the operation as a "declaration of vengeance" rather than the start of a broader war reflected careful messaging designed to satisfy domestic political demands for forceful action while avoiding commitments to expanded military involvement in Syria. The operation''s timing—just days after the December 17 Dover ceremony and within Trump''s first year back in office—highlighted the administration''s emphasis on swift, decisive military responses as a central component of its national security strategy.


  The success of Operation Hawkeye Strike in degrading ISIS capabilities remained subject to ongoing assessment, with military officials indicating that follow-up strikes and sustained operations might be necessary to fully eliminate the ISIS presence in targeted areas. The operation demonstrated the complex challenges of combating insurgent groups in Syria''s desert regions, where ISIS has maintained a persistent presence through guerrilla tactics and exploitation of ungoverned territories.
