The Pentagon announced a formal investigation into Senator Mark Kelly (D-AZ), a retired U.S. Navy captain, for "serious allegations of misconduct" after he appeared in a video with five other Democratic lawmakers urging military service members to refuse unlawful orders. Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth stated Kelly could face recall to active duty for court-martial under the Uniform Code of Military Justice.

Trump called the lawmakers "traitors" and accused them of "seditious behavior at the highest level," demanding they be "arrested and put on trial." Hegseth explained Kelly was targeted because he remains subject to UCMJ as a retired officer, unlike the other civilian lawmakers in the video.

The investigation represents an unprecedented use of military law to threaten a sitting member of Congress for constitutionally protected speech. Legal experts called such a potential court-martial "beyond the pale" and "an abuse of power." The only comparable case dates to 1925 when Army Col. Billy Mitchell was court-martialed for insubordination.

The action chills congressional oversight of the military and suggests retaliation against lawmakers who question administration policies. Kelly's video simply reminded troops of their existing legal obligation to refuse clearly unlawful orders—a principle established at Nuremberg and enshrined in military law.
