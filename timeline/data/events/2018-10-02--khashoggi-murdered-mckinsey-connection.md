Saudi journalist Jamal Khashoggi is murdered by a 15-member squad of Saudi operatives at the Saudi consulate in Istanbul, Turkey, on orders from Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman according to CIA and UN investigations. The assassination occurs in the context of McKinsey's 2016 dissident identification report that named Omar Abdulaziz, a close friend and political ally of Khashoggi, as one of three influential Saudi critics on Twitter. After McKinsey's report identified Abdulaziz, Saudi authorities hacked his phone, compromising his private communications with Khashoggi and providing intelligence about their coordinated opposition to MBS. Abdulaziz later testifies that after the McKinsey report, Saudi authorities 'imprisoned and tortured' his family, friends and associates, with his younger brother having teeth pulled out and others waterboarded and electrocuted. The Khashoggi murder exposes the deadly consequences when Western consulting firms provide authoritarian regimes with intelligence on dissidents, even if ostensibly for 'internal use.' Turkish investigators, the CIA, and a UN investigative team all conclude Khashoggi's assassination was likely ordered by Mohammed bin Salman. Senator Elizabeth Warren calls out McKinsey's role in a letter to the firm's Managing Partner: 'I am concerned that McKinsey's report on public perception may have been weaponized by the Saudi government to crush criticism of the Kingdom's policies.' The murder represents the most extreme outcome of McKinsey's willingness to serve authoritarian clients while maintaining U.S. government contracts.
