By December 2025, at least a dozen people who received clemency from Trump across both terms had been arrested for separate crimes, highlighting the consequences of bypassing the traditional pardon review process.

Notable re-arrests include: Andrew Taake, a January 6 pardoned defendant who spent two weeks as a fugitive before pleading guilty to online solicitation of a minor and receiving a 3-year sentence. Brent Holdridge, another January 6 defendant, was re-arrested in May 2025 for alleged theft of industrial copper. Daniel Ball Woods was convicted in April 2025 of aggravated DUI and reckless homicide in a wrong-way crash, sentenced to 17 years in August.

Two recipients of Trump's first-term commutations demonstrated the pattern: Eliyahu Weinstein, whose sentence was commuted in 2021, was convicted in March 2025 of a new $35 million fraud scheme and sentenced in November 2025 to 37 years. Jonathan Braun, also commuted in 2021, was convicted of multiple charges including sexual abuse and sentenced in November 2025 to 27 months.

Legal experts noted the high recidivism rate resulted from Trump not following the usual pardon review process that evaluates candidates' likelihood of rehabilitation. The pattern demonstrates how mass clemency without vetting releases dangerous individuals who go on to harm others.
