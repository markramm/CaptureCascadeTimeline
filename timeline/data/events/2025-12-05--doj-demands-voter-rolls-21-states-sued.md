Since May 2025, the DOJ has demanded full voter registration files from at least 40 states and Washington DC, including confidential information like driver's license numbers and partial Social Security numbers. By mid-December, the DOJ had sued 21 states and DC for refusing to comply.

States sued include California, Colorado, Delaware, Georgia, Hawaii, Illinois, Maine, Maryland, Massachusetts, Michigan, Minnesota, Nevada, New Hampshire, New Mexico, New York, Oregon, Pennsylvania, Rhode Island, Vermont, Washington, and Wisconsin. At least 11 Republican-led states agreed to comply, including Alabama, Mississippi, Missouri, Montana, Nebraska, South Carolina, South Dakota, Texas, Tennessee, Utah, and Virginia.

The DOJ claims authority under the Civil Rights Act of 1960, but critics argue the demand violates state privacy laws and risks political targeting of voters. The DOJ sent confidential memoranda of understanding requiring states to remove voters flagged as "ineligible" during federal review. Federal officials confirmed plans to share voter data with DHS for citizenship verification.

The campaign represents an unprecedented federal effort to centralize control over voter information, potentially enabling mass voter purges based on flawed federal databases. Constitutional scholars warn it tramples the constitutional delegation of election administration to states and creates infrastructure for voter intimidation.
