CBS/Paramount announced the cancellation of The Late Show with Stephen Colbert, the highest-rated late-night program for nine consecutive seasons. The cancellation came days after Colbert described Paramount's $16 million settlement with Trump as "a big fat bribe" and while Paramount sought FCC approval for its Skydance merger.

Trump celebrated the cancellation on Truth Social, posting that Colbert "got what he deserved" for years of critical coverage. The timing—immediately after Colbert's criticism of the Paramount settlement—suggested the cancellation was retaliatory.

Paramount's pending merger with Skydance required FCC approval, giving the Trump administration significant leverage over the company. Critics noted the settlement with Trump and subsequent Colbert cancellation appeared designed to curry favor with an administration that controlled the merger's fate.

The cancellation sent a chilling message throughout media: criticism of Trump or his business dealings carries professional consequences when companies need government approvals. Media analysts described it as the most significant example of corporate self-censorship since the administration began, with a top-rated show sacrificed to maintain regulatory relationships.
