President-elect Donald Trump agreed to $25 million settlement for fraudulent Trump University operation ten days before trial, reversing his repeated vows never to settle. New York AG Eric Schneiderman's $40 million 2013 lawsuit alleged Trump University engaged in systematic consumer fraud, falsely promising "secrets of success" in real estate while delivering high-pressure sales tactics for expensive courses taught by unqualified instructors. The settlement distributed $21M to class action participants, $3M to New York victims, and $1M penalty for operating an unlicensed university - recovering approximately 90% of costs for 7,000 defrauded students. Trump admitted no wrongdoing despite clear evidence. The case revealed regulatory capture: Texas AG Greg Abbott quashed $5.4M lawsuit in 2010 after Trump University contacted his office; Florida AG Pam Bondi's office dropped investigation days after Trump Foundation donated $25K to her reelection campaign. The pattern demonstrates how wealthy individuals use predatory educational schemes to exploit aspiring middle-class victims while political connections shield them from accountability.
