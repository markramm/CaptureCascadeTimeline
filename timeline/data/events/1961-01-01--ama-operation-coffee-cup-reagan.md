The American Medical Association launches Operation Coffee Cup, a sophisticated grassroots lobbying campaign against proposed Medicare legislation that marshalls the AMA Woman's Auxiliary to organize coffee meetings featuring a vinyl LP recording of actor Ronald Reagan speaking against "socialized medicine." The campaign distributes packs to doctors' wives containing letter templates and Reagan's 10-minute recording "Ronald Reagan Speaks Out Against Socialized Medicine," charging them with drumming up support by organizing coffee mornings where they play the LP to "stimulate friends and neighbors" to write letters to Congress objecting to the King-Anderson bill championed by President Kennedy. Reagan's recording criticizes Social Security for supplanting private savings, warns that subsidized medicine would curtail Americans' freedom, cites expansion of private health insurance and the 1960 Kerr-Mills Act as evidence that King's legislation is unnecessary, and concludes the bill is "simply an excuse to bring about what they wanted all the time: socialized medicine." The campaign proves largely successful, inundating senators and representatives with objections and leading to defeat of the Medicare bill in the Senate in 1961, demonstrating effective corporate lobbying infrastructure mobilizing grassroots opposition to social programs. This 1961 campaign precedes the Powell Memo by 10 years but exemplifies the sophisticated lobbying techniques Powell would later urge all business to adopt, including grassroots organization, media messaging, and political mobilization. The campaign also launches Reagan's political career, with him registering as Republican in 1962 and delivering his famous 1964 Republican National Convention address. Operation Coffee Cup represents pre-Powell corporate political infrastructure successfully defeating progressive legislation through coordinated lobbying.
