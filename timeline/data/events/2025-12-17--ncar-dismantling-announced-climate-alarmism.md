OMB Director Russ Vought announced plans to "break up" the National Center for Atmospheric Research (NCAR) in Boulder, Colorado, calling it "one of the largest sources of climate alarmism in the country." NCAR is a 60-year-old federally funded research center employing approximately 830-1,000 scientists, established by the National Science Foundation in 1960.

Vought stated that "a comprehensive review is underway & any vital activities such as weather research will be moved to another entity or location." The announcement came with no prior notice to NCAR leadership or the University Corporation for Atmospheric Research that operates the facility.

Congressional representatives suggested the move was political retaliation against Colorado Governor Jared Polis for refusing to pardon Tina Peters, convicted of illegally accessing voting machines. NCAR develops critical forecasting tools including the Weather Research and Forecasting Model (WRF) used globally by meteorologists and emergency managers.

The American Geophysical Union called the plan "senseless and dangerous," while the American Meteorological Society warned dismantling would "hurt the economic health of the nation and the safety of its people." The attack on NCAR represents the administration's escalating war on climate science and the institutions that produce inconvenient research.
