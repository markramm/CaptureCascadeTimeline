In Ukraine, McKinsey & Company and Paul Manafort - later President Trump's campaign chairman and subsequently convicted of financial fraud - are paid by the same oligarch to help burnish the image of disgraced presidential candidate Viktor Yanukovych, recasting him as a reformer tilting toward the West. The oligarch financing both efforts is Rinat Akhmetov, Ukraine's richest oligarch, who rescues Yanukovych through a strategy including hiring two very different consulting groups. Manafort's job is twofold: to soften Yanukovych's reputation and strengthen his Russian-leaning Party of Regions, facilitating his rise to the presidency. McKinsey provides something different - an economic plan that Yanukovych can use to portray himself as a market-based reformer with Western orientation. Once in office, Yanukovych rebuffs the West, sides with Russia, and eventually flees the country accused of stealing hundreds of millions of dollars. More than 80 protesters are killed before Yanukovych flees Ukraine in 2014. Outraged, Putin pushes into Ukraine, annexes Crimea and stirs up a war that claims more than 10,000 lives. McKinsey defends its role by saying the foundation was serious about promoting economic development in Ukraine and featured prominent Westerners on its board. The engagement demonstrates how McKinsey's technocratic veneer of market-based reform can be deployed to legitimize fundamentally corrupt and anti-democratic figures, with the firm's 'neutral' economic expertise weaponized to deceive Western observers about the true nature of authoritarian clients.
