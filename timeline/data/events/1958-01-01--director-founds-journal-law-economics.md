Aaron Director founds the Journal of Law and Economics at the University of Chicago, creating the premier academic publication platform for disseminating Chicago School legal and economic theory that favors corporate interests over regulation. The journal institutionalizes law and economics as a legitimate field of legal scholarship, providing credibility and distribution for research that challenges government regulation and promotes market solutions to legal problems. Ronald Coase joins Director as co-editor in 1963, using the editorship to encourage research advancing their vision of law through an economic lens that typically favors efficiency over equity and corporate freedom over regulatory constraint. The journal plays an important role in forming the field of law and economics, publishing influential articles on economic analysis of regulation, behavior of regulated firms, political economy of legislation and legislative processes, law and finance, corporate finance and governance, and industrial organization. Director's journal helps unite law and economics with far-reaching influence, training generations of legal scholars to apply market-fundamentalist economic theory to legal questions, producing scholarship that judges and policymakers cite to justify deregulation and reduced antitrust enforcement. This 1958 founding precedes the Powell Memo by 13 years but implements Powell's later strategy of establishing "staff of scholars" whose work would provide intellectual justification for corporate-friendly legal doctrines. The Journal of Law and Economics becomes the flagship publication for academic work that reshapes American law to favor corporate power, demonstrating that the infrastructure Powell called for in 1971 was already being systematically built by corporate-funded academics throughout the 1950s and 1960s.
