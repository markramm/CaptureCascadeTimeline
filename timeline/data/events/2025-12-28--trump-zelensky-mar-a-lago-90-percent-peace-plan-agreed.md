President Trump hosts Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky at Mar-a-Lago for a three-hour meeting on a peace plan to end the Russia-Ukraine war. Trump claims they covered "95%" of issues and are "maybe very close" to a deal. Zelensky states that 90% of a 20-point peace plan is agreed, with US-Ukraine security guarantees "100% agreed" and military dimensions finalized.

The most contentious remaining issue is territory. Moscow demands Ukraine withdraw from all of eastern Donetsk region, while Ukraine currently controls less than 50% of Donetsk. Trump warns Zelensky that more land "may be taken over the next period of a number of months and you're better off making a deal now." The US proposed transforming disputed areas into "free economic zones" with three-way control between the US, Ukraine, and Russia.

Critically, Ukraine would no longer seek full NATO membership under the deal—a significant concession from Kyiv's long-standing position. The US offers 15-year security guarantees (Zelensky wants 50 years), but these would NOT include US troops on the ground. Europe would provide forces and fund most new assistance.

Trump signed a defense bill on December 18 providing $400 million annually for Ukraine in fiscal years 2026-2027—dramatically down from the $67 billion total during the Biden era. The meeting represents a fundamental shift in US Ukraine policy from unlimited support to pressuring territorial concessions that would validate Russia's invasion.
