Federal judges in Colorado, Washington D.C., and Illinois issue rulings blocking ICE's systematic warrantless arrest practices as unconstitutional violations of the Fourth Amendment.

In Colorado, Judge R. Brooke Jackson issues a 66-page ruling declaring ICE "routinely" makes unlawful warrantless arrests, finding that mere presence in the U.S. without authorization is insufficient to justify warrantless arrest. He orders ICE to establish probable cause of flight risk before making arrests.

In Washington D.C., Judge Beryl Howell bars warrantless arrests without establishing probable cause of flight risk. Her ruling cites White House officials setting a 3,000 daily arrest quota and references Stephen Miller telling agents to "just go out there and arrest" people. Court documents show 943 arrests occurred in D.C. between August 7 and September 9 alone.

In Illinois, a judge extends the 2022 Castañon Nava consent decree through February 2, 2026, ordering release of people subjected to warrantless arrests and requiring ICE to retrain officers who violated the consent decree. Already 600 people have been ordered released for warrantless arrest violations.

The rulings reveal an administration prioritizing arrest quotas over constitutional rights. Multiple federal courts independently finding the same pattern of systematic Fourth Amendment violations demonstrates that the unconstitutional conduct is policy, not aberration.
