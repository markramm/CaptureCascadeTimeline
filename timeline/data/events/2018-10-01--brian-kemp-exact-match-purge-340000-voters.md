Georgia Secretary of State Brian Kemp, the Republican candidate for governor in a high-stakes race against Democrat Stacey Abrams (who would become the first African American woman governor of any state), presided over a massive voter purge while refusing to recuse himself from his election oversight role. An investigation by journalist Greg Palast found that Kemp improperly purged 340,134 voters from registration rolls on false grounds that they had moved—but they actually still lived at their registered addresses. Voters were removed from 'inactive' to 'canceled' status based solely on missing the 2014 and 2016 elections and failing to return a postcard. In July 2017, Kemp's office had already removed 560,000 people from voter rolls for 'infrequent voting'—an unusually high number that Democrats alleged was election manipulation. In the final month of the campaign, Kemp's 'exact-match' policy held up 53,000 pending registrations, mostly people of color, over minor discrepancies like missing apostrophes or hyphens in names. Lawsuits charged that 50,000 blocked registrations were 80% Black, Latino, or Asian voters. Purged voters were more likely to live in Democratic precincts: nearly 47% came from precincts that Abrams carried by 10+ percentage points. Kemp defeated Abrams by approximately 55,000 votes—fewer than the number improperly purged. The blatant conflict of interest exemplified election manipulation through secretaries of state refusing to recuse themselves.
