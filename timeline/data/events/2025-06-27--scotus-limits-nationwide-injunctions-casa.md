The Supreme Court ruled 6-3 in Trump v. CASA, Inc. that federal courts lack authority to issue universal or nationwide injunctions blocking policies across the entire country. Writing for the majority, Justice Barrett held that the Judiciary Act of 1789 only authorizes party-specific remedies.

While the Court didn't rule on the constitutionality of Trump's birthright citizenship executive order itself, the decision severely limits judicial oversight of executive actions. Lower courts can no longer block unconstitutional policies nationwide—they can only protect the specific plaintiffs before them.

The ruling represents a major expansion of executive power and reduction in judicial checks. Previously, when one court found a policy unconstitutional, the injunction could halt enforcement everywhere while appeals proceeded. Now, the administration can continue implementing potentially unconstitutional policies against everyone except named plaintiffs in each lawsuit.

The practical effect: the administration can violate constitutional rights of millions while litigation proceeds case by case, court by court, making judicial review of executive overreach far less effective as a check on power.
