The Supreme Court ruled 6-3 to allow enforcement of Trump's Executive Order 14168 "Defending Women from Gender Ideology Extremism," which restricts passport sex designations to "male" or "female" based on biological sex at birth, eliminating the "X" marker for nonbinary individuals.

The Court stayed a Massachusetts district court preliminary injunction that had blocked the policy. Justice Jackson's dissent warned that passports listing only gender at birth make transgender people vulnerable to "increased violence, harassment, and discrimination" and that "This Court has once again paved the way for the immediate infliction of injury without adequate (or, really, any) justification."

The ruling forces transgender Americans to carry government documents that contradict their lived identity and appearance, creating dangerous situations at border crossings, airports, and any interaction requiring ID. Nonbinary Americans lose the ability to have official documents that accurately reflect their identity.

The decision represents another instance of the Court's conservative majority enabling discrimination against LGBTQ+ individuals while deferring to executive authority to restrict civil rights.
