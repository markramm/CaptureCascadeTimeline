White House AI and Crypto Czar David Sacks is shaping federal technology policy while holding more than 700 private investments, including hundreds in AI companies. The New York Times found Sacks remains invested in 449 companies with AI products through his venture capital firm Craft Ventures.

Sacks received financial conflict of interest waivers from the White House allowing him to work on policy affecting his investments. Critics say the waivers grant "carte blanche" to violate conflict of interest statutes. His firm Craft Ventures invested in crypto company BitGo, which benefited from the GENIUS Act that Sacks backed. After the legislation passed, BitGo filed for an IPO.

Sacks has weakened national security controls on chip exports and brokered a UAE AI deal while maintaining financial stakes in affected industries. Senator Elizabeth Warren is investigating whether Sacks exceeded his 130-day term limit as a special government employee, which would subject him to stricter ethics requirements.

The arrangement represents textbook regulatory capture: a venture capitalist with hundreds of millions invested in AI and crypto companies is now the primary architect of federal policy in those sectors. Every policy decision he makes—from chip export controls to crypto regulation to AI governance—directly affects the value of his personal investments.
