Just days after President Trump takes office and issues executive orders on January 25, 2017 ordering 'all legally available resources' to be shifted to border detention facilities and calling for hiring 10,000 new immigration officers, ICE redirects McKinsey's existing consulting contract toward executing the White House's immigration crackdown. McKinsey consultants are tasked with two core objectives: advising on hiring 10,000 new immigration officers and reducing costs of handling an expected influx of migrant detainees.

This pivot transforms McKinsey's Obama-era 'organizational transformation' work into direct support for Trump's deportation machine. The consulting team's focus shifts from general efficiency to measurable metrics of deportation speed and detention cost reduction. McKinsey consultants begin analyzing ICE operations through the lens of 'quantifiable benefits' measured primarily in 'increased total removals and reductions in time to remove a detainee,' as later stated in ICE contracting documents.

The redirection marks a critical moment where a corporate consulting contract crosses from bureaucratic optimization to becoming an instrument of controversial political policy. McKinsey consultants, trained to optimize for numerical targets and cost efficiency, are now applying those methods to human beings in detention facilities, setting the stage for recommendations that would shock even career ICE officials.

Trump's executive orders represent his implementation of campaign promises to crack down on illegal immigration. The orders specifically call for expanding detention capacity, expediting removal proceedings, and increasing enforcement personnel. McKinsey's role is to translate these political directives into operational reality, optimizing the machinery of deportation for speed and efficiency.
