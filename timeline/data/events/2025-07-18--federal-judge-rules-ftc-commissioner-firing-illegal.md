Federal Judge Loren AliKhan ruled that President Trump's firing of FTC Commissioner Rebecca Slaughter was illegal and ordered her reinstatement. The decision represented one of the few successful judicial checks on Trump's efforts to purge independent agency leadership.

Judge AliKhan found that Slaughter's termination violated the FTC Act's "for cause" removal protection, which allows commissioners to be removed only for "inefficiency, neglect of duty, or malfeasance in office." Trump had fired Slaughter for policy disagreements, which the court held was insufficient cause.

The ruling rejected the administration's argument that the president has unlimited power to remove any executive branch official. AliKhan noted that Congress deliberately structured independent agencies to operate free from political pressure on enforcement decisions.

The decision, while a victory for agency independence, faced immediate appeal. The administration argued the case should be expedited to the Supreme Court, seeking to establish presidential removal authority over all agency heads. Legal observers noted the case could eventually result in the overturning of Humphrey's Executor and the end of independent regulatory agencies.
