The Transportation Security Administration began providing airline passenger manifest data to Immigration and Customs Enforcement multiple times per week, transforming domestic air travel into a warrantless surveillance checkpoint for immigration enforcement.

The data sharing program allows ICE to cross-reference passenger lists against immigration databases and target individuals for arrest at airports. Civil liberties advocates warn the program effectively requires domestic travelers to submit to immigration screening without probable cause or judicial oversight.

The arrangement expands surveillance beyond traditional border zones into the interior United States, treating any domestic flight as an opportunity for immigration enforcement. Legal experts note the program circumvents Fourth Amendment protections by using administrative data sharing rather than traditional law enforcement requests.

The TSA-ICE coordination represents a broader pattern of repurposing security infrastructure built for counterterrorism toward immigration enforcement. Critics argue the program chills travel by immigrant communities and transforms airports into de facto internal checkpoints—a significant expansion of the surveillance state.
