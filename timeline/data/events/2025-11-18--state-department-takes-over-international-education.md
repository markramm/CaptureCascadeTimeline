The State Department's Bureau of Educational and Cultural Affairs assumed control of Title VI area studies programs and Fulbright-Hays international education initiatives as part of the Trump administration's plan to dismantle the Department of Education.

The transfer moved programs that fund international studies at American universities, language training, and scholarly exchanges from an education-focused agency to one primarily concerned with diplomacy and foreign policy. Critics warned the shift would subordinate educational goals to political priorities.

Title VI programs have supported the study of less commonly taught languages and world regions at American universities since 1958, while Fulbright-Hays has funded faculty research abroad. Both programs contribute to American expertise on foreign regions crucial for national security, business, and diplomacy.

The reorganization raised concerns about politicization of scholarly programs, given the State Department's more direct connection to administration foreign policy. Area studies scholars worried that programs critical of allied governments or supportive of perspectives at odds with administration policy could face defunding or restructuring.
