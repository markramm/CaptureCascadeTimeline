The Biden FTC published its final rule banning hidden "junk fees" in the Federal Register ten days before Trump's inauguration, requiring businesses to disclose all mandatory fees upfront. The rule targets practices like hotel resort fees, concert ticket service charges, and other surprise fees that cost consumers billions annually.

Trump-appointed Commissioner Andrew Ferguson dissented on the rule's timing rather than substance, signaling that while he supported the policy goal, he opposed the rushed implementation. His dissent provided a preview of how Trump's FTC would approach consumer protection—supporting industry-favorable enforcement priorities.

The rule's future remains uncertain under the incoming Trump administration, which promised sweeping deregulation. Industry groups immediately filed legal challenges, and the Trump FTC could choose not to defend the rule vigorously or could initiate proceedings to repeal it.

The episode illustrates the vulnerability of last-minute regulatory protections: even popular consumer-friendly rules face an uphill battle when the incoming administration is ideologically opposed to federal regulation and aligned with the industries being regulated.
