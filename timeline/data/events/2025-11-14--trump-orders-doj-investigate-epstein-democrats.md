President Trump publicly directed Attorney General Pam Bondi to investigate Jeffrey Epstein's ties to Bill Clinton, Larry Summers, Reid Hoffman, and J.P. Morgan Chase—despite Epstein's records also documenting extensive ties to Trump himself.

Bondi immediately complied, assigning Jay Clayton (U.S. Attorney for the Southern District of New York) to lead the investigation. The directive openly violated DOJ's post-Watergate principle of independence from White House direction on specific investigations.

This followed earlier Trump orders to Bondi resulting in indictments of New York AG Letitia James and former FBI Director James Comey—both of whom Trump had personal grievances against. The pattern demonstrates selective prosecution: pursuing political enemies while ignoring comparable or greater connections among allies.

The selective focus on Democratic Epstein connections while ignoring Republican ties illustrates weaponization of federal law enforcement. Trump had his own documented relationship with Epstein, calling him a "terrific guy" in 2002 and hosting him at Mar-a-Lago. The investigation targets Trump's political opponents while shielding his allies, transforming DOJ into an instrument of political persecution.
