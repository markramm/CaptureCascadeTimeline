McKinsey announces it will end its contract with Immigration and Customs Enforcement following internal employee pressure and public outcry over the firm's role in Trump's immigration crackdown. The decision comes as McKinsey faces mounting criticism for helping ICE implement harsh detention and deportation policies during the family separations scandal.

McKinsey's new managing partner, Kevin Sneader, conveys the decision in a note to employees stating the ICE work had 'rightly raised' concerns and that McKinsey 'will not, under any circumstances, engage in any work, anywhere in the world, that advances or assists policies that are at odds with our values.' The announcement follows internal protests by McKinsey consultants who were disturbed by the firm's role in optimizing deportation processes and proposing cuts to food and medical care for detainees.

However, McKinsey's claimed ethical awakening is undercut when, just one week later, the firm signs a new $2 million contract with Customs and Border Protection, another immigration enforcement agency. A number of McKinsey's most controversial proposals for ICE, including the funding cuts for food and medical care that even ICE officials found too harsh, are never implemented.

The ICE contract scandal exemplifies the ethical failures of management consulting: firms take lucrative government contracts without considering the moral implications of their work, face public backlash only when exposed by investigative journalism, make performative statements about values, then continue similar work under different contracts. By the time McKinsey ends the ICE engagement, the firm has earned more than $20 million from immigration enforcement work that helped enable Trump's cruelest policies.
