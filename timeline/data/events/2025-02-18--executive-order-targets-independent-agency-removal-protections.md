President Trump signed an executive order asserting presidential authority to remove the heads of independent regulatory agencies at will, attacking a 90-year precedent that protects agencies like the FTC, SEC, and NLRB from political control.

Since Humphrey's Executor v. United States (1935), independent agencies have operated with commissioners who can only be removed "for cause"—meaning misconduct or incapacity, not policy disagreements. This independence allows agencies to make decisions based on expertise and law rather than political pressure.

The executive order directed that all agency heads serve at the president's pleasure, regardless of statutory removal protections. The order anticipated legal challenges and instructed the Justice Department to argue that Humphrey's Executor should be overturned.

Legal scholars warned the order would transform independent agencies into political instruments, allowing presidents to fire commissioners who investigate allies or pursue enforcement against donors. The order particularly threatened agencies like the FTC (antitrust), SEC (securities), and NLRB (labor rights) where independence from political interference is most critical.
