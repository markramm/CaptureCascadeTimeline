In July 2017, McKinsey & Company entered into a contract with the fiscal oversight board to draw up 'detailed privatization/corporatization plans' for Puerto Rico's Electric Power Authority (PREPA) and the Highways and Transportation Authority, setting the stage for electricity grid privatization that would devastate Puerto Rico's infrastructure resilience. Four oversight board members wrote in a June 2017 Wall Street Journal op-ed that 'only privatization will enable PREPA to lower costs and provide more reliable power throughout the island'—despite PREPA's chronic underinvestment stemming from decades of debt service to Wall Street bondholders. McKinsey was simultaneously advising the board while its subsidiary MIO Partners held investments in Puerto Rico bonds, creating conflicts where privatization recommendations could benefit the firm's investment portfolio. The consulting firm counted Quanta Services as a client—the same company that would later form LUMA Energy joint venture to take over PREPA's transmission and distribution. Just two months after this contract, Hurricane Maria would strike Puerto Rico in September 2017, exposing the catastrophic consequences of decades of infrastructure neglect driven by debt service priorities. Rather than rebuilding PREPA as a resilient public utility, McKinsey's privatization framework would accelerate the transfer of Puerto Rico's electrical infrastructure to private corporations. The timing demonstrated classic 'shock doctrine' tactics: using crisis and austerity to impose privatization schemes that served financial interests while dismantling public assets. McKinsey earned at least $120 million in fees for advising the oversight board, including this privatization planning that directly benefited its corporate clients and potentially its own investment holdings.
