Three days after the deadly Unite the Right white supremacist rally in Charlottesville, Virginia—where a neo-Nazi murdered Heather Heyer by driving into counter-protesters—President Trump held a press conference and declared: 'I think there is blame on both sides. You had some very bad people in that group, but you also had people that were very fine people, on both sides.' The Unite the Right rally (August 11-12, 2017) had brought together alt-right, neo-Confederates, neo-fascists, white nationalists, neo-Nazis, and Klansmen protesting removal of a Robert E. Lee statue. Trump added: 'Not all of those people were neo-Nazis, believe me. Not all of those people were white supremacists by any stretch.' The comments drew widespread condemnation from Democrats and many Republicans who said Trump failed to adequately condemn white supremacists and created a false equivalence between protesters and counter-protesters. Biden later cited Trump's remarks as the reason he decided to run for president, saying Trump 'assigned a moral equivalence between those spreading hate and those with the courage to stand against it.' Critics noted Trump's explanation didn't hold up because he referenced protests 'the night before' when white nationalists had burned tiki torches while chanting anti-Semitic and white nationalist slogans including 'Jews will not replace us.' The incident demonstrated Trump's willingness to provide cover for white nationalist violence, signaling to extremist groups they had protection from the presidency.
