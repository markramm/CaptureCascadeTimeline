The ACLU of Minnesota files a federal class action lawsuit against ICE and the Department of Homeland Security over "Operation Metro Surge," alleging systematic violations of constitutional rights against observers and bystanders.

Six Minnesota residents allege ICE agents pepper-sprayed peaceful observers, pointed assault rifles at bystanders, temporarily abducted U.S. citizens, followed people home in unmarked cars to intimidate them, and detained a U.S. citizen despite proof of citizenship in violation of the Fourth Amendment.

A second ACLU lawsuit filed December 18 targets Freeborn County to stop ICE cooperation, claiming the county's policy led to civil rights violations and deportations for minor violations like broken tail lights.

The lawsuits document ICE targeting constitutional rights to observe and document law enforcement activities—a fundamental First Amendment protection. The use of military-style tactics including assault rifles and pepper spray against civilians exercising their right to observe represents an escalation of force against constitutionally protected activity.

The pattern of detaining U.S. citizens despite proof of citizenship demonstrates the over-aggressive nature of enforcement operations driven by arrest quotas rather than actual immigration violations. At least 170 U.S. citizens have been wrongfully detained in the first nine months of the Trump immigration crackdown.
