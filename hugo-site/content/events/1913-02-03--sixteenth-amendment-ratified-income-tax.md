The Sixteenth Amendment to the U.S. Constitution is ratified by Delaware, completing ratification by 36 states and establishing Congress's right to impose a federal income tax without apportionment among states based on population. The amendment, passed by Congress on July 2, 1909, and officially proclaimed by Secretary of State Philander Knox on February 25, 1913, directly responds to the 1895 Supreme Court decision in Pollock v. Farmers' Loan & Trust Co. that had struck down previous income tax attempts. This marks the first constitutional amendment since the Fifteenth Amendment in 1870 and enables the Revenue Act of 1913, which implements progressive taxation to address wealth concentration. The amendment represents a critical victory for Progressive Era reformers seeking to shift tax burden from tariffs and excise taxes that disproportionately affected working people to direct taxation of high incomes and corporate profits, creating a new fiscal framework that would partially fund the growing federal government and social programs throughout the 20th century.
