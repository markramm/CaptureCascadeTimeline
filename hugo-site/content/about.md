## Our Mission

This timeline documents the systematic capture of American democratic institutions through verifiable events, court records, and public reporting. We are building a living intelligence infrastructure that helps citizens, journalists, and researchers understand and respond to the erosion of democratic safeguards.

## The Capture Cascade Framework

Democracy doesn't fall gradually — it **cascades**. Each captured institution enables the capture of more, creating exponential acceleration. This framework helps explain the accelerating erosion documented in the timeline:

### The Pattern

1. **Crisis Creation:** Real or manufactured crises generate public fear and demand for immediate action
2. **Emergency Powers:** Leaders claim extraordinary authority to "solve" the crisis, bypassing normal checks
3. **Regulatory Capture:** Industries write their own rules through favorable appointments and gutted oversight
4. **Accountability Elimination:** Watchdogs fired, inspectors general removed, whistleblowers silenced
5. **System Capture:** The new corrupt normal becomes entrenched, making future corruption easier

### How Events Are Categorized

Events are assigned to **capture lanes** (Institutional Capture, Judicial Capture, Executive Power Expansion, etc.) based on which democratic mechanism they affect. An event may appear in multiple lanes if it impacts multiple systems simultaneously.

The cascade stage assignment reflects the *function* of the event in the broader pattern, not a claim about intent. For example, firing an inspector general is categorized as "Accountability Elimination" because it removes a check on power — regardless of the stated reason for the firing.

### What Would Count as Counter-Evidence

This framework is falsifiable. Counter-evidence would include:

- **Sustained institutional restoration** — captured agencies regaining independence and function over multiple years
- **Accountability mechanisms self-correcting** — inspectors general, courts, or oversight bodies successfully checking executive overreach without external pressure
- **Deceleration in capture events** — a sustained multi-year decline in documented events rather than the acceleration pattern we observe
- **Capture reversal without crisis** — democratic norms restored through normal institutional processes rather than through elections, litigation, or public pressure

We actively look for these patterns. Where restoration events occur (e.g., court injunctions blocking executive overreach, judicial appointments restoring balance), they are documented in the timeline alongside capture events.

### The Evidence

Our timeline shows the mathematical reality of this cascade:

- **1970-2000:** ~8 events/year
- **2001-2016:** ~28 events/year
- **2017-2020:** ~68 events/year
- **2021-2026:** ~95 events/year

This is not gradual erosion — it is exponential acceleration.

## Methodology

### Source Tiering

Every source is assigned a tier based on reliability:

| Tier | Standard | Examples |
|------|----------|---------|
| **Tier 1** | Wire services, papers of record, government primary sources | AP, Reuters, NYT, WaPo, PBS, NPR, court filings, Federal Register, congressional testimony |
| **Tier 2** | Established outlets with editorial standards | The Hill, Axios, Politico, CNBC, Fortune, Military.com, state news outlets |
| **Tier 3** | Analysis, opinion, or advocacy with documented factual basis | Lawfare, ProPublica investigations, academic papers, think tank reports |

Events require a **minimum of 2 credible sources** (tier 1 or tier 2). Single-source events are flagged with `status: reported` rather than `status: confirmed` until corroborated.

### Event Status

- **Confirmed**: Corroborated by 2+ credible sources
- **Reported**: Single credible source; awaiting corroboration
- **Developing**: Ongoing events where facts are still emerging
- **Disputed**: Events where credible sources disagree on key facts

### Importance Rating

Events are rated 1-10 for importance based on their **impact on democratic institutions**, not political alignment:

- **9-10**: Structural changes to democratic systems (e.g., constitutional challenges, nuclear strikes, mass institutional purges)
- **7-8**: Significant erosion of specific institutions or norms
- **5-6**: Notable events that contribute to broader patterns
- **3-4**: Contextual events that document ongoing trends
- **1-2**: Minor events included for completeness

### What We Do Not Document

- Unverified claims or rumors
- Events documented only by partisan sources lacking editorial standards
- Policy disagreements that do not involve institutional capture mechanisms
- Events outside the scope of democratic institutional erosion

### Corrections

If we make a mistake, we correct it transparently. Corrections are tracked in the git history of each event file. If you find an error, please [report it](https://github.com/markramm/CaptureCascadeTimeline/issues).
