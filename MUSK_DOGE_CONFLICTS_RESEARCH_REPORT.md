This research report documents verified conflicts of interest involving Elon Musk's role as co-chair of the Department of Government Efficiency (DOGE) from January-May 2025, and how government decisions during this period benefited his business empire, which has received at least $38 billion in government contracts, loans, subsidies, and tax credits over 20 years. - **$38 billion in total government support** to Musk companies since 2003

- **Multiple investigations ended or stalled** after Trump took office
- **Key regulatory personnel fired** from agencies overseeing Musk companies
- **Billions in new contracts** awarded to Starlink and SpaceX during DOGE's operation

## 1. EVAN FEINMAN RESIGNATION - BEAD PROGRAM FAVORITISM

### Background
Evan Feinman served as Director of the $42.5 billion Broadband Equity, Access, and Deployment (BEAD) program at the Commerce Department's NTIA until March 14, 2025.

### Key Events

**March 16, 2025**: Feinman announced his departure with a scathing warning email to colleagues about Starlink favoritism.

### Feinman's Statement
> "Stranding all or part of rural America with worse internet so that we can make the world's richest man even richer is yet another in a long line of betrayals by Washington."

### The Conflict
- **Original BEAD rules**: Starlink expected to receive approximately $4.1 billion
- **Post-DOGE review**: Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick pledged to make program "technology neutral," potentially awarding Starlink **$10-20 billion**
- **Feinman's concern**: Satellite internet is inferior to fiber for long-term infrastructure

### Verified Impact
The BEAD program represents a "generational investment" in rural broadband. Feinman warned that low spending caps would force states to choose cheaper satellite options over fiber, directly benefiting Starlink while providing inferior service to rural Americans.

## 2. SEC CASES AND ROBIN ANDREWS RESIGNATION

### SEC Lawsuit Against Musk

**January 14, 2025**: Six days before Trump's inauguration, the SEC sued Elon Musk for securities violations related to his Twitter/X acquisition.

### The Allegations
- Musk failed to disclose his 9.2% Twitter stake on time - filed 21 days late on April 4, 2022
- SEC alleges Musk saved "at least $150 million" by underbuying stock during the delay
- Filed in U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia under Judge Sparkle L. Sooknanan

### Timeline of Case
- **January 14, 2025**: SEC files lawsuit
- **March 14, 2025**: Process server delivers summons to Musk at SpaceX headquarters in Brownsville, Texas
- **March 30, 2025**: SEC announces proceeding with lawsuit; deadline set for Musk response by June 6
- **June 6, 2025**: Musk receives 6-week extension; new deadline July 18, 2025

### Robin Andrews Resignation

**April 4, 2025**: Robin Andrews, the top SEC attorney on the Musk case, resigned, calling it a "heartbreaking day."

### Andrews' Statement
> "The SEC must always be focused on protecting investors and holding violators accountable, no matter who they are."

### Reasons for Resignation
According to sources familiar with the matter:
- Andrews expressed concerns the SEC under Trump-appointed leadership would dismiss the case or settle for minimal penalty
- His resignation came days after DOGE entered SEC offices
- Came after Musk taunted the SEC on X about the enforcement action

### Additional Context
- Bernard Smyth, another attorney on the Musk lawsuit, also left the agency in early April 2025
- Trump administration reversed 15-year SEC policy on formal investigation orders, slowing future probes
- SEC offered $50,000 buyouts to employees to encourage resignations/retirements by March 21

## 4. VERIZON FAA CONTRACT CANCELLATION

### The Contract at Stake

**Value**: $2.4 billion over 15 years (awarded to Verizon in 2023)

**Purpose**: Overhaul communications system serving as backbone of nation's air traffic control system

### February 2025: FAA Considers Cancellation

The FAA came close to canceling the Verizon contract and awarding work to Starlink, according to two people briefed on the plans.

**Options under consideration:**
1. Outright cancellation of Verizon contract
2. Let Verizon continue while handing some work to SpaceX

### Current Status
- FAA testing one Starlink terminal in Atlantic City
- Two terminals at non-safety critical sites in Alaska
- FAA considering Starlink for telecommunication connections and weather information at remote sites

### SpaceX Response
SpaceX denied "takeover" reports, stating:
> "Recent media reports about SpaceX and the FAA are false."

SpaceX claims it's working "in coordination" with L3Harris Technologies (the prime contractor) to test Starlink "as one piece of the infrastructure upgrades."

### Congressional Response

**Ranking Member Larsen:**
> "If the FAA cancels a long-term contract with Verizon in favor of Starlink while ignoring long-established protocol, that would stink of corruption. Our Committee must get to the bottom of what the hell is going on between the FAA and Starlink."

### Conflict of Interest
This represents a significant test of conflicts of interest given Musk's role as head of DOGE, which was formed to slash federal spending. The decision would directly benefit Musk's company while he simultaneously oversees government efficiency efforts.

**Musk determines his own conflicts**: It has been left up to Musk himself to decide if he needs to step out when DOGE work overlaps with his private interests.

- Financial disclosures remain confidential (permitted under law)

## 8. STRATEGIC CRYPTOCURRENCY RESERVE LEGISLATION

### Executive Order - Strategic Bitcoin Reserve

**March 6, 2025**: President Trump signed Executive Order establishing:
1. **Strategic Bitcoin Reserve** - treats bitcoin as reserve asset
2. **U.S. Digital Asset Stockpile**

**Capitalization:**
- Reserve capitalized with bitcoin from criminal/civil asset forfeitures
- U.S. government currently holds **207,000+ Bitcoin** (worth approximately $17 billion as of March 15, 2025)

**David Sacks (White House AI and Crypto Czar):**
> Strategic Bitcoin Reserve would be "like a digital Fort Knox for" cryptocurrency

### The BITCOIN Act

**Introduced by**: Senator Cynthia Lummis

**Provisions:**
- Establishes Strategic Bitcoin Reserve into law
- Capitalizes from seized digital assets
- Allows Treasury to directly purchase **1 million Bitcoin**
- Acquire approximately **5% of total Bitcoin supply**

### The GENIUS Act (Stablecoin Legislation)

**Signed into law by President Trump (July 2025)**

**Purpose**: Sets federal standards for stablecoins (cryptocurrencies pegged to fiat currencies)

**Requirements:**
- "Permitted payment stablecoin issuers" must hold reserves equivalent to every dollar
- Permitted reserves: insured bank deposits, short-term Treasury bills, central bank reserves

### Additional Legislation

**Digital Asset Market Clarity Act:**
- Creates regulatory regime for cryptocurrency classification
- Determines whether tokens are securities (SEC) or commodities (CFTC)

**Anti-CBDC Surveillance State Act:**
- Prohibits Federal Reserve from issuing digital currency

### Political Response

**Republicans**: Generally favorable toward pro-crypto legislation

**Democrats**: Less supportive

**Maxine Waters (Ranking Member, House Financial Services Committee):**
Called Bitcoin reserve and digital asset stockpile "silly"
